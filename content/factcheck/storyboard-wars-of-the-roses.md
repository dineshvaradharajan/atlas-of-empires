## Areas (used sparingly; the base map carries the crown)
- **Yorkist-held Calais**, Oct 1459 – 1460: Warwick was Captain of Calais and the garrison stayed loyal to him against Somerset (Wars of the Roses article).
- **Yorkist-held Dublin and the Pale**, Oct 1459 – 1460: York, Lieutenant of Ireland, took refuge there, and Warwick sailed there to plan with him. The ring is the border data's own Pale outline.
- **Yorkist-held London and the south-east**, from the Sandwich landing (26 Jun 1460), London (2 Jul), and after Northampton (10 Jul) the Home Counties as far as Northampton, until the base map turns Yorkist (1461). This shows that the Yorkists held London and the captive king while Henry VI was still the crowned king.
- **Lancastrian-held north of England**, Dec 1460 – 28 Mar 1461: Margaret's lords "massed their armies in the north" (Wakefield article), and York was in Lancastrian hands in January 1461 (heads on Micklegate Bar, Edward IV article). It is drawn until the eve of Towton, because the base map turns Yorkist on 1 January 1461 although Edward was proclaimed only on 4 March.
- **Readeption**: spreads from Warwick's landing at Dartmouth and Plymouth (13 Sep 1470) to all of England by 3 Oct 1470 (border data's England outline), held until Edward IV retook London on 11 Apr 1471 (the area ends 10 Apr).
- **Tudor England**, 22 Aug – 31 Dec 1485: the war file makes England Yorkist for all of 1485.
- **Simnel's Dublin and the Pale** from the fleet's arrival (4 May 1487) and his coronation (24 May).

## Maps traced
No campaign atlas was needed for areas: the rings reuse the border data's own England and Pale outlines, and the hand-drawn north
and south-east areas follow county lines. Arrow routes follow the dated stops in the battle articles: Royston and Ware 1455;
Middleham, Blore Heath and Ludlow; Sandwich, London and Northampton; Mill Bay, Shrewsbury, Nottingham and Leicester; Furness,
Bramham Moor, Tadcaster, York, Nottingham and Newark.

## Uncertainties
- Calais and Ireland in 1470–71 (Calais went over to Henry VI under Warwick's deputy) are not drawn, for lack of a fetched source, so they stay Yorkist during the Readeption. The Isle of Man shape follows the crown colour.
- Arrow dates for Warwick's 1459 crossing, Edward's flight to King's Lynn (late Sep 1470) and the Lancastrian retreat after Second St Albans are approximate.
- Casualties are infobox ranges. Barnet's (c. 1,000 Yorkist and up to 10,000 Lancastrian) is flagged in the text as varying widely. Bosworth's Yorkist losses are unknown, and casNum 1,000 is a placeholder middle. Wakefield follows recent research (a skirmish, 12–15 Yorkists killed by one estimate).

## Warnings left (8)
All are arrows that end in ground held by the other house. In a dynastic war with no fronts that is what an attack looks like: York's march on St Albans (1455), Warwick's and Salisbury's marches to Ludlow (1459), York's march to Sandal (1460), Margaret's march to St Albans (1461), her march to Tewkesbury (1471), and Lincoln's march to Stoke (1487). The eighth, Edward's flight to King's Lynn, ends at sea on his way to Burgundy, which is neutral ground.
