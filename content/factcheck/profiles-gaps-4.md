## Unverifiable / disputed

- Nanda start -345: kept. It follows the Wikipedia infobox (Habib and Jha, from the Sri Lankan 22-year tradition) and Raychaudhuri's 345 BCE coronation. Alternatives: 382 (Pargiter), 364 (Mukherjee; Upinder Singh gives "364/345"), 424 (astronomical theory), and Merutunga's 467. The summary already says "though some scholars date him earlier". Peak.year -345 equals the start year; the Taagepera (1978) figure of c. 1.0M km² for Magadha is for 350 BCE (Table 2, 2nd-largest column), so the pairing is approximate. Puranic reign lengths for Mahapadma (88 or 28 years) do not fit a 345 BCE start, which shows the chronology is uncertain.
- Mahameghavahana start -170: kept, matching the founder date in the dynasty article's infobox (Mahameghavahana 170 BCE … Kharavela 50 BCE). The same article also says "2nd or 1st century BC" and uses 3rd-century-BCE categories, so the start is a convention, not a firm date. The map draws this realm only for -170 to -165.
- Mahameghavahana end 320 (was 350): "early 4th century CE" rests on one cited paper (Sahu 1977). The later "Meghas" of South Kosala are said to have ruled into the 6th century. Any single year here is a rough estimate.
- Pushyamitra–Kharavela war: the e-PG Pathshala (INFLIBNET) page states it without hedging, but Raychaudhuri and Sircar reject it. It is kept only as a doubted theory.
- Shunga reigns: Pushyamitra is c. 185–149 in his own article and 185–151 in the Shunga Empire article, so Agnimitra is 149 or 151 – 141. Kept with "c.".
- Pala end 1161: Madanapala's reign is 1144–1162 (Majumdar) or 1143–1161. The Pala Empire infobox gives 750–1161. Kept "around 1161". Pala reign dates differ by historian (for example Devapala 810–850 Majumdar, 815–850 infobox, 821–861 Chowdhury). The profile follows Majumdar.
- Sur rulers: the Wikipedia ruler tables contradict each other (two "Adil Shah" entries; Sikandar Feb–Jun 1555). The Sikandar Shah reign "1555" refers to his time at Delhi; he held out in the Punjab until he surrendered at Mankot in 1557.
- Greco-Bactrian: Taagepera 1979's 2.5M km² for 184 BCE is confirmed by Wikipedia's List of largest empires and the kingdom infobox. Taagepera 1978 Table 2 gives Bactria 1.8M (200 BCE) and 2.0M (150 BCE), as the note says. The map itself draws only c. 1.4M km². Start -250 against the infobox's 256 BCE: kept, since the article dates the secession c. 250, with a high chronology of c. 255 and a low one of c. 246.
- Indo-Scythians: Taagepera 1979's 2.6M km² for 20 CE is confirmed by the List of largest empires. The map draws only c. 1.5M km², including the Western Satraps. Whether the Azes era equals the Vikrama era (58/57 BCE) is debated; the summary's "often equated" is kept.
- Harsha: Taagepera (1997) gives 1.0M km² for 625 ("peak size reached?") and 648; Turchin, Adams & Hall (2006) give 1.00M for 648. Both confirmed. Taagepera dates Harsha's death to 648, while most sources give 647. "Died without an heir": the minister Arunasva took Kannauj, so there was no dynastic heir. Kept.
- Rashtrakuta: Takkolam c. 949 was not re-read in the Rashtrakuta dynasty article. It is the standard date and is in the cited Krishna III article.
- Escholarship Taagepera PDFs return HTTP 403 to automated fetches, but their text was read from local copies (Taagepera 1978 Table 2; Taagepera 1997 appendix). The URLs are the same ones the existing profiles use. They are kept.
