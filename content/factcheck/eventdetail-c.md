# Event details log: slice C, early-modern and modern empires (`content/journeys/c-modern.json`)

**Details written: 198 of 198 events** in 15 journeys (all events covered). Length 83–115 words (mean c. 99), 3–5 sentences each. Each one gives the context, what happened and the consequence for the empire's territory or power.

Method: Britannica returns HTTP 403 to automated fetches, as the journeys-c log already notes. So every detail was checked against English Wikipedia articles fetched in this session (about 230 pages), mostly the specific event or treaty article: its lead, infobox and cited body text. Claims that the fetched text did not support were removed or reworded. Examples: Henry Hudson's 1609 voyage, Yermak's "800 men", the Cape-to-Cairo motive for the 1890 Ultimatum, and the Habsburg war during Abbas I's 1603 campaign. Only `detail` was added, plus one `text` fix (below). No `year`, `lonlat`, order or other field was changed (verified by a field-by-field diff against HEAD). New pages used were appended to each journey's `sources`, and every journey now has 8 sources.

## `text` corrections
- [soviet-union, 1922] text: "Treaty on the Creation of the USSR signed on 30 December" → "Treaty on the Creation of the USSR approved on 30 December". Reason: the infobox gives the treaty as signed on 29 December 1922 and effective on 30 December, and the text says the conference of delegations approved it on 30 December. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Creation_of_the_USSR

## Contested or uncertain facts hedged
- Kosovo 1389: who won is disputed. The detail says "reliable accounts are scarce and early reports even disagreed on who had won", and credits Murad's killing to Miloš Obilić only "traditionally".
- Ankara 1402: army sizes are given as "perhaps 140,000 against no more than 85,000" (modern estimates; the chronicles inflate them).
- Chaldiran 1514: Ottoman strength is given as the range "an estimated 60,000 to 100,000".
- Mamluk conquest 1517: when the Sharif of Mecca submitted varies by source, so the detail gives no date for it.
- Yermak: the Sibir campaign is "about 1581–82", and his death is "1584 or 1585 (sources differ)".
- Pereiaslav 1654: whether it was an alliance or a submission "is still disputed by Ukrainian and Russian historians".
- Bartolomeu Dias: furthest point given as Kwaaihoek near the Boesmans River (not the Great Fish River, as commonly said). Cape sighted on the return voyage, May 1488.
- Cabral 1500: "Whether the landfall was an accident or planned is still debated."
- Cajamarca 1532: Pizarro's force is "fewer than 200" (sources give 168–180).
- Ayacucho 1824: strengths are disputed (5,780–8,500 patriots, 6,906–9,310 royalists), so the detail says "a royalist army of similar or larger size".
- Treasure voyages: 317 ships, 62 treasure ships and about 27,800 men are given "according to Ming records" (the figures are debated).
- Jingnan 1402: no month given (sources differ between June and July 1402). The Jianwen Emperor "probably" died in the palace fire.
- Malwa 1561: Sarangpur is dated 29 March 1561, and Mughal rule is said to be "completed by 1562", because sources give both years.
- Asirgarh 1601: taken "by some accounts through trickery rather than assault".
- Bahadur Shah II: exiled to Rangoon "later" (no date given). The article gives December 1857, other accounts 1858.
- Plassey 1757: strengths are given as "about 3,000" and "around 50,000".
- Nader's sack of Delhi 1739: "some 3,000" Persian soldiers killed in the riot, as one account gives it. No total is given for the massacre.
- Partition 1947: deaths are given as the range 200,000–2 million, and 12–20 million people displaced.
- Taiping Rebellion: estimates of the dead "range from 20 to 100 million".
- Qing peak c. 1790: "by scholarly estimates some 14.7 million km²" (matches the event text and the journeys-c log).
- Herero and Nama: "an estimated 40,000 to 80,000 Herero and 10,000 Nama". The "first genocide of the 20th century" label was dropped because the fetched article does not state it.
- Soviet–Afghan War: Soviet dead are given as "between about 14,500 and 26,000".
- Sambhaji 1689 and Shahu 1707: Shahu is "crowned at Satara on 12 January 1708". The release in 1707 and the accession in 1708 are kept distinct, matching the journeys-c note.
- Third Anglo-Maratha War: the Peshwa "surrendered in June 1818" (one infobox ends the war in 1819).
- Ottoman sultanate abolition and Lausanne: dates follow the Wikipedia article. The empire is described as lasting "since about 1299".
- Peace of Utrecht: Spain's Italian and Netherlands losses are described as "under the Utrecht treaties and the related Treaty of Rastatt", because the transfers to Austria came at Rastatt.
- Molotov–Ribbentrop: the pact is described as "dated 23 August" (it was signed in the early hours of 24 August).
