# Fact-check log: Defining Moments 1500–2024 (content/moments-ce-b.json)

54 moments, 1500–2024, ascending by year, unique ids, categories from the allowed list. Every
brief item is covered (9/11 and the 2001–2021 Afghanistan war are one combined entry, as the brief
grouped them). Additions beyond the brief: Qing conquest of Beijing (1644), First Fleet / Australia
(1788, the Oceania entry, with the Treaty of Waitangi in "also"), Darwin (1859), Battle of Adwa (1896),
the Holocaust (1941–45) and China's reform and opening-up (1978). Continents covered: Europe, Asia,
Africa (slave trade, Berlin Conference, Adwa, Year of Africa, end of apartheid), North America,
South America (Inca conquest, Latin American independence, plus Potosí in "also"), Oceania.

## Sources
All 126 cited URLs were fetched while writing and re-checked (HTTP 200, no redirects) on 2026-11-09.
Britannica refused automated fetches (HTTP 403), so it is not cited. Sources are English Wikipedia
(article text read for each cited statement) plus institutional pages: US National Archives, UN (History
of the UN; UN Charter), USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia, US State Department Office of the Historian,
Federal Reserve History (1929 crash; Great Recession), NASA (Apollo 11), W3C (Tim Berners-Lee),
WHO (14.9 million excess deaths release) and the Nobel Prize site. CERN's "birth of the web" page
returned 404 and South African History Online returned a 404 page, so neither is cited.

## Figures and dates: which estimate was used
- WWII deaths: Wikipedia now gives 60–75 million (many older sources say 70–85 million). Used 60–75 million.
- WWI deaths: 15–22 million (WWI article and WWI casualties article; the casualties page flags this line
  "failed verification", May 2026). Kept as the mainstream range.
- Napoleonic Wars: Wikipedia's infobox totals are 1.8M+ coalition and 1.0M+ French/allied military dead;
  Bell and Kennedy (cited there) suggest up to 5 million military deaths including disease.
  Wrote "at least 2.8 million to as many as 5 million".
- Seven Years' War: "roughly 900,000" is my sum of the national military-death figures in the
  Wikipedia infobox (Prussia 180k, Britain 160k, France 200k, Habsburg 165k, Russia 120k, Spain 29k+,
  Sweden 28k; Portugal unknown). No published total was found, so the text says "by one compilation of
  national estimates".
- Thirty Years' War: 4.5–8 million. Taiping Rebellion: 20–30 million (some estimates are higher).
- Atlantic slave trade: 12.5 million embarked and 10.7 million arrived (Anderson et al. 2013, via Wikipedia).
  Manning's figures in the same article are about 12 million and 10.5 million.
- Indigenous Americas: pre-contact estimates range from 8 to 100 million, with many scholars favouring
  around 50 million. Decline 80–95% (Columbian exchange article; the "great dying" study cited there says ~90%).
- Partition of India: 200,000–2 million deaths (disputed; Talbot & Singh); 12–20 million displaced.
- Korean War: about 1 million military deaths and 1.5–3 million civilian deaths.
- Vietnam: lede gives 970,000–3 million Vietnamese dead and the infobox 966,000–3,010,000; used the lede.
  US deaths are 58,220 (DoD figure in the lede; the infobox says 58,281).
- Hiroshima and Nagasaki: 150,000–246,000 dead by the end of 1945.
- Soviet–Afghan War: 1–3 million Afghan deaths; Soviet dead 14,453–26,000.
- Afghanistan 2001–2021: 176,000–212,000+ dead, including 46,319 Afghan civilians (Costs of War).
- COVID-19: about 7.1 million confirmed deaths; WHO excess estimate of 14.9 million (range 13.3–16.6M)
  for 2020–21 only; 19.1–36 million is the modelled range Wikipedia gives as of January 2023.
- 1918 influenza: 17–50 million deaths, possibly 100 million; earliest probable cases in Haskell County,
  Kansas, March 1918. The origin is still undetermined, so the pin is labelled "earliest probable recorded cases".

## Disputed or softened
- Magellan–Elcano: 18 men returned on the Victoria in September 1522. Wikipedia also says "about 40 men"
  completed the circumnavigation overall, counting captives released later. The text says 18 aboard the Victoria.
- Inca: Huáscar is called Atahualpa's "brother"; the fetched text didn't say whether they were half-brothers.
- Fall of Beijing: Wikipedia says 24 April 1644 (often given elsewhere as 25 April). The text says "April 1644".
- Westphalian sovereignty: described as a scholarly reading that some historians call a later invention,
  as the Peace of Westphalia article says.
- Enlightenment as inspiration for the French Revolution: supported by the article's "key events" and its
  framing, not by an explicit sentence. The American link is explicit ("critical precursor").
- Berlin Conference: dropped the common claim that "no Africans were present" and that its borders "are
  still in use", because neither was in the fetched text.
- Haiti: the text no longer says the rebels "defeated French, Spanish and British forces". Louverture was
  allied with Spain early on, so it now says "13 years of war and shifting alliances".
- Mandela's release: the fetched text says only "1990" (de Klerk's speech was on 2 February 1990), so the text
  says "that year". 11 February 1990 is widely reported but wasn't in a fetched page.
- World Wide Web: Wikipedia gives both 6 August 1991 (infobox, first published) and 23 August 1991
  (released to the whole Internet). The text says "August 1991".
- Korean armistice "never replaced by a peace treaty": inferred from the armistice article (a 1954 peace
  treaty proposal was not adopted; the armistice holds "until a final peaceful settlement").
- Cuban Missile Crisis: the atmospheric test-ban treaty is named without its year (1963), which wasn't in the fetched text.
- Russian Revolution dates are given in New Style, with Old Style in brackets for 7 November / 25 October.
- Ethiopia "also": the Italian occupation from 1936 is sourced. That it ended during WWII is mainstream,
  but the end date (1941) wasn't in the fetched text, so no year is given.

## Year and pin choices for the editor
- columbian-exchange: year 1500 is a nominal placeholder for "1492 onwards", to keep it inside this
  slice (1492 falls in the other writer's period).
- atlantic-slave-trade: year 1560 is nominal (Wikipedia says the trade to the Americas began in the 16th
  century and the Portuguese began shipping to Brazil "around 1560"). The Luanda pin is illustrative: no
  fetched page named a single main port, so the place says "pin: Luanda, Angola".
- Symbolic pins: Warsaw (WWII), Delhi (Partition), Lagos (Year of Africa), Havana (Cuba), Fort Sumter
  (US Civil War, where it began), Kennedy Space Center (Apollo 11 launch), London (Principia, published
  under the Royal Society's imprimatur; Newton's Cambridge base wasn't in a fetched page), Gonaïves
  (Haitian independence declared there), World Trade Center (9/11 + Afghanistan). Kabul is already used for
  the Soviet–Afghan War.
- vietnam-war uses its start year, 1955, as the other wars do (WWI 1914, WWII 1939, Korea 1950), so it sorts before 1960.
- Categories: the pandemics (1918, COVID-19), the 1929 crash, the 2008 crisis and the end of the USSR
  are "collapse". The Holocaust is filed under "war" because there is no genocide category; an editor
  may prefer another one.
