# Event details log: slice B, medieval empires (`content/journeys/b-medieval.json`)

**Details written: 250**, one for every event in all 19 journeys (72–110 words each, 3–5 sentences, average 88 words).

Method: Britannica refused automated fetches (HTTP 403), as in the earlier journeys pass. So the details rest on English Wikipedia articles, fetched as plain text through the Wikipedia API, with only cited statements used. World History Encyclopedia pages (already in the sources) were used for Mali (Sundiata takes the Ghana capital, 1240) and the Inca (Topa Inca doubles the empire; Chimú artists brought to Cusco). No `year`, `lonlat`, order or other field was changed. New pages were added to `sources` where they were used, up to the cap of 8.

## `text` corrected
- **tang-dynasty 640**: "Conquest of Gaochang (Turfan); Four Garrisons of Anxi set up" → "Conquest of Gaochang (Turfan); Anxi Protectorate set up". The Protectorate was set up in 640 at Turfan. The Four Garrisons (Kucha, Khotan, Kashgar, Karashahr) came only after the conquest of Karashahr and Kucha in 648, and were installed in 648–658 according to one article and in 649 according to another. Only one passage in the Tang dynasty article says "installed in 640". Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_General_to_Pacify_the_West, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_campaigns_against_the_Western_Turks

## Contested facts hedged in the details (text left unchanged)
- **Huns 433**: Aetius ceded only "some parts" of Pannonia; both the date (431/433) and the extent are disputed. **435**: Margus is dated "the following year" after Rugila's death, per the Attila article. **454**: the 30,000 dead at Nedao is Jordanes' figure.
- **Byzantine 1369**: the fall of Adrianople is given as 1361/1362 by some historians and as c. 1369 by others (Edirne article). **1018**: the 15,000 blinded prisoners are given as the chroniclers' figure.
- **Tang 657**: the Tang dynasty article puts Su Dingfang's victory at Issyk Kul, while the Battle of Irtysh River article puts it on the Irtysh. The detail gives both. **751**: the claim that Talas brought papermaking west is marked as doubted.
- **Tibet 640**: Wencheng left China in 640 (Tibetan Empire article) or 641 (Princess Wencheng article); both are given. **645**: Zhangzhung's conquest is dated 634 (Tang annals), 645 (Stein), or placed under Trisong Detsen. **812**: Kabul's submission is dated c. 812 or 815. **838**: Ralpachen was killed by Langdarma or by pro-Bön ministers. **842**: Langdarma's assassination is sometimes dated 846.
- **Rashidun 637**: Umar received Jerusalem in 637 or 638. **711**: Tariq is called "probably a Berber" (his origin is debated). **739**: the Berber Revolt broke out in 739 (Umayyad Caliphate article) or 740 (Berber Revolt article).
- **Abbasid 813**: Baghdad fell in Sept 813, but unrest went on until al-Ma'mun entered the city in 819; the detail says so. **1258** (also Mongol 1258): the story of books thrown into the Tigris is given as a later account, since Biran (cited in the siege article) shows that libraries reopened within two years.
- **Carolingian 751**: the proclamation at Soissons is given as "traditionally".
- **Ghana 830**: al-Khwarizmi's mention is dated "about 830" (the map is dated 833). **990**: Awdaghust became Ghanaian in 990 (Ghana Empire article) or "late 10th or early 11th century" (Aoudaghost article). **1076**: the Almoravid conquest is presented as a tradition rejected by Conrad and Fisher (1982). **1154**: al-Idrisi's reliability is questioned (Levtzion and Spaulding).
- **Khmer 1006**: Suryavarman I's reign starts in 1002 (his article) or 1006 (Khmer Empire article). **802**: the meaning of "Java" is debated. **1431**: the seven-month siege comes from Thai chronicles, and Angkor was not wholly abandoned.
- **Chola 885**: Thirupurambiyam is dated c. 885 (Chola Empire article) or 878 (Aditya I article). **925**: the Chola Empire article dates the Sri Lankan conquest to 925, while the Parantaka I article dates the main invasion and the sack of Anuradhapura to c. 946–947. **1017**: the date follows the Mahavamsa (1017–18).
- **Seljuk 1092**: the Assassins are blamed for Nizam al-Mulk's murder by "most accounts", and Malik-Shah was "possibly poisoned".
- **Mongol 1241**: Ögedei's death is not the only explanation for the withdrawal. **1259**: Möngke died of disease by most accounts; one article says he was killed in battle.
- **Delhi 1303**: the Padmini legend is marked as rejected by most historians. **1327**: the move to Daulatabad as protection against the Mongols follows Ibn Battuta. **1330s peak**: Muhammad bin Tughluq ruled "one of the largest" areas (the peak date is disputed; see corrections.json). **1414**: the Sayyids' claimed descent from the Prophet is doubted.
- **Mali 1240**: the capture of the Ghana capital is dated by WHE only. **1285**: Sakura's accession date is Delafosse's estimate, and his slave origin is uncertain. **1312**: Musa's Atlantic-voyage account may be invented. **1325**: the submission of Gao and Timbuktu is doubted by modern historians. **1599**: the Mali Empire article marks this as the end; the empire broke up c. 1610.
- **Aztec 1375**: the chronicles date Acamapichtli's accession anywhere from 1350 to 1376. **1472 and 1479**: the Aztec Empire article dates the Toluca campaign to 1472 and the Purépecha defeat to 1479, while the Axayacatl article gives 1474 and 1476. Both are given. **1520**: Moctezuma died "in unclear circumstances".
- **Inca 1527**: Huayna Capac's death from smallpox is disputed. **1533**: Atahualpa's execution is dated 29 August "according to most accounts" (26 July is also cited).
- **Songhai 1465**: Mema's conquest is dated 1465 by the Mali Empire article, while the Sonni Ali article says Songhai already held Mema. **1468**: Timbuktu fell in 1468 (or 1469, by the Sonni Ali article). **1473**: Djenné fell in 1473 (one passage gives 1475), and the seven-year siege is tradition. **1496**: the hajj is dated 1496 or 1497–98. **1502**: Diafunu fell in 1502 (Mali article) or 1501 (Askia Muhammad I article). **1590**: estimates of the Moroccan army range from 4,000 to 20,000.
