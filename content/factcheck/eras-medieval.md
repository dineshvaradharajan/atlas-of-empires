## Unverifiable / disputed

- Thule migration timing (1000, 1100, 1200 chapters): kept as written (eastward spread from c. 1000, Greenland in the 1200s), which matches Wikipedia's Thule article. I recall (not checked against a fetched source) that some radiocarbon studies, such as Friesen & Arnold 2008, put the eastward migration later, in the 1200s; treat "beginning to expand east" at 1000 as the early end of the range.
- East Polynesian settlement: corrected to the short chronology of Wilmshurst et al. 2011 (Society Islands c. 1025–1120, Hawaii/Rapa Nui/New Zealand c. 1190–1290). Wikipedia's Polynesians article still quotes an older, longer chronology (Hawaii by 900, Rapa Nui by 1000), so these dates remain debated.
- Great Zimbabwe at 1000: "early stone-walled settlement is beginning" left in place. Wikipedia says stone building began in the 11th century, but other accounts date the first walls to the 13th century (not checked against a fetched source).
- Gwanggaeto's reign (400 chapter): given as 391–413 (Korean reckoning). Wikipedia's infobox gives 391–412; left unchanged.
- Siege of Constantinople 674–678 (700 chapter): this is the traditional dating. Some recent scholarship argues for a series of blockades rather than a single siege (from background knowledge, not checked against a fetched source). Left unchanged.
- Nagabhata II at 800: accession is dated variously c. 795 or c. 800 (predecessor Vatsaraja c. 780–800). Left unchanged.
- Rulers left as already hedged: Kushan ruler in 100 (Vima Takto or Vima Kadphises), Khazar khagan Busir "(c.)" in 700 (attested 688–711), Mali's Keita rulers in 1279–1400, Niani as Mali's capital ("c."), and the founding of Kongo (c. 1390s; origin traditions were written down much later).
- Teotihuacan population figures ("perhaps 100,000" at 200 and 300): estimates range from 60,000–80,000 early on to 100,000–200,000 at the peak (c. 450). Kept because the text already hedges with "perhaps".
- Baghdad c. 800 at "perhaps half a million": a common estimate. I found no fetched source that pins it down; kept because it is hedged.
- I could not fetch Britannica or Met Museum pages (403 / not attempted after the Britannica block), so sources are mostly English Wikipedia articles whose statements are themselves cited.
