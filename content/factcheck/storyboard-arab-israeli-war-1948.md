# Storyboard: 1948 Arab–Israeli War (`content/cinema/arab-israeli-war-1948.json`)

Sides follow `content/wars/d-recent.json`: **0 = Israel (Jewish forces before May 1948), 1 = Palestinian Arab forces, 2 = Arab League states**.
The Arab Liberation Army (ALA) in Galilee is drawn on side 1 (irregular forces inside Palestine), the Arab Legion, Egyptian, Iraqi, Syrian and Lebanese armies on side 2.

## Chapters (7) and why
1. **Partition and civil war** (29 Nov 1947 – 31 Mar 1948): UN vote, civil war, roads and Jerusalem besieged; little territory changed.
2. **April–May 1948: the Haganah offensive** (Plan Dalet): Nachshon, Tiberias, Haifa, Safed, Beisan, Jaffa; Gush Etzion falls; flight and expulsions; 14 May declaration.
3. **The Arab armies invade** (15 May – 11 Jun): Egypt to Isdud and Bethlehem, Arab Legion in the Old City and at Latrun, Iraq in Samaria, Syria at Degania and Mishmar HaYarden; Burma Road; first truce.
4. **The Ten Days** (8–18 Jul): Operation Dani (Lydda, Ramle) and Dekel (Nazareth); Latrun holds.
5. **Operations Yoav and Hiram** (15 Oct – 5 Nov): Beersheba, the Faluja pocket, Isdud/Majdal evacuated; upper Galilee and the southern Lebanon strip.
6. **Operation Horev** (22 Dec 1948 – 7 Jan 1949): Auja, the incursion into Sinai and withdrawal.
7. **The armistice lines of 1949**: agreements with Egypt, Lebanon, Transjordan, Syria; Uvda to Umm Rashrash; Faluja evacuated; Wadi Ara transferred; Green Line, West Bank under Transjordan, Gaza Strip under Egypt.

Wording: both names are given (War of Independence / Nakba); flight and expulsions are stated with mainstream figures (Lydda and Ramle 50,000–70,000 per the Operation Dani article; about 750,000 overall per the war article) without loaded terms.

## Method
- Control is painted per date as layers (Jewish/Israeli blocks, Egyptian, Jordanian/Iraqi, Syrian and ALA areas) on a 1 km raster, then compared with the corrected base map of the year: an occupation ring is drawn only where the base map shows the wrong side (e.g. Arab-held Galilee in 1948, when the base map already draws Israel at its 1949 extent; West Jerusalem, which the coarse base map puts inside the West Bank; the Egyptian-held Gaza Strip, which the corrections cut out of Israel; the southern Lebanon strip and the Sinai foothold). Rings are traced from the raster, so neighbouring areas share edges exactly.
- Two fronts where lines existed: the Egyptian line Isdud–Negba–Faluja–Beit Jibrin (29 May – 21 Oct 1948) and the central line Latrun–Ras al-Ein–Wadi Ara (15 May 1948 – Jul 1949, moving east after Operation Dani and after the Wadi Ara transfer), with hold keys every ≤5 weeks.

## Uncertainties
- The civil war was a patchwork of towns and villages: Jewish settlement blocks are drawn as areas; Jaffa and Arab Haifa fall in the sea on the base map's coarse coastline and cannot be shown (mentioned in text only).
- The southern Negev was largely unheld until Operation Uvda (March 1949); where no force is painted the base map (Israel from 1948) shows through.
- The base map gives a strip east of the Jordan and Hula to Mandatory Palestine in 1948; it is painted Syrian.
- Egypt's advance up the coast (15–29 May) is shown with an interim Arab-held Majdal–Isdud belt.
- The Wadi Ara transfer date (drawn 20 May 1949) is approximate; the 1949 Auja area (later a demilitarised zone) shows as Israeli-held because the base map draws it inside Egypt.

## Warnings left
- Chapters 2 and 7 "little visible change": Plan Dalet's gains were mostly towns inside blocks already Jewish-held, and the 1949 changes (Faluja, Wadi Ara, the Lebanese strip) are small at map scale.
- Seven "pocket" warnings (600–1,860 km²), all real or base-map artefacts: the Jewish Negev settlement block inside Arab-held country (Jan 1948); the Hula panhandle and the Beisan valley between Syrian and Jordanian areas; Egyptian-held Sinai between the Israeli Sinai foothold and the Auja area; base-map Jordan and Egypt slivers beside Palestinian-held areas.
