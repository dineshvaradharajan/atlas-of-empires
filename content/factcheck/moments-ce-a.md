# Fact-check log: Defining Moments 1–1500 CE (content/moments-ce-a.json)

40 moments, ascending by year, ids unique and not clashing with content/moments.json. Every inhabited continent
has map points: Europe (incl. Constantinople), Asia (East, South, Central and West), Africa (Aksum, Timbuktu,
Great Zimbabwe), North America (Cahokia), Mesoamerica (Teotihuacan, Tikal, Tenochtitlan), the Caribbean
(Columbus), South America (Cusco) and Oceania (Society Islands).
All sources were fetched during writing. Wikipedia's API was rate-limited (HTTP 429), so pages were read
through WebFetch. PNAS (pnas.org) and the British Library refused automated fetches (403/404), so the
Wilmshurst paper is cited through its PubMed Central copy and Magna Carta through The National Archives (UK).

## Dates adjusted or chosen
- christianity-spreads: year 33 follows the Early Christianity article's c. 33 for the crucifixion (many scholars give 30 or 33).
  Label "c. 33–65 CE" ends with Paul's death in Rome, c. 64/65. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle
- buddhism-china: 68 CE is the traditional founding of the White Horse Temple; the source says many scholars treat the
  Emperor Ming story as a fable and the temple is not attested before 289. Label "1st–2nd century CE"; firm evidence is
  An Shigao from 148. The often-cited 65 CE reference to Prince Ying of Chu was not on the fetched pages, so it is omitted.
- teotihuacan: label "c. 100–550 CE" (Pyramid of the Sun complete by c. 100; elite buildings burned c. 550). The city was
  founded c. 100 BCE and may have lasted into the 7th–8th c. Population hedged: c. 100,000 (range 25,000 to 150,000+).
- cai-lun-paper: earlier paper dated from the Fangmatan fragment, c. 179–141 BCE (History of paper). The Cai Lun article says
  "as early as the 3rd century BCE"; the more specific, earliest-surviving-fragment date was used. Does not duplicate the
  existing BCE "paper" moment; it is framed as the 105 CE refinement.
- pax-romana: year 117 (Trajan's death, greatest extent); label gives the traditional 27 BCE–180 CE span. Population 70 million
  is a single estimate; the Wikipedia claim that this was "33% of the world" was left out.
- kushan-kanishka: 127 follows Falk's accession date; the older 78 CE (Saka era) dating is no longer accepted.
- constantine: Edict of Milan is dated 13 February 313; the source notes no formal "edict" may ever have been issued (the text is
  a letter of Licinius), so the entry says they "agreed at Milan". Constantinople consecrated 11 May 330.
- gupta-age: label "c. 320–550 CE": Chandragupta I crowned 320 (dynasty from c. 240); last main-line ruler to 550, though the
  infobox ends the empire c. 579. "Golden age" flagged as disputed. Aryabhata dated "around 500" (he records being 23 in 499).
- aksum-christian: Ezana's conversion is given as 325 or 328 (Kingdom of Aksum) and only "320s–c. 360 reign" (Ezana article);
  label "c. 325–340 CE". Frumentius is called Phoenician in one place and Syrian in another, so the entry says "a Christian from
  the Roman east". "First coins in sub-Saharan Africa" not supported by the source, so not claimed.
- fall-western-rome: 4 September 476 (Fasti vindobonenses priores); one source gives 31 August. Alternative end dates (480) noted.
- justinian: plague dated 541 (first appearance in Egypt); it peaked in Constantinople in 542. Spanish reconquest 552.
- hijra: 622 (departure May 622; Hijri epoch 16 July 622). Mecca's surrender given as 629–630 (the Muhammad article gives
  December 629 in its lead and 630 in its timeline). "Unified Arabia" softened to "most of the peninsula had converted".
- zero-brahmagupta: 628, Brahmagupta's work. The fetched page says he was born in Bhillamala but does not say where he
  wrote it, so the entry calls him "a native of Bhillamala"; the map point is Bhinmal. Fibonacci's 1202 date is not used
  (not on the fetched page); "from the 12th century" is used instead.
- arab-conquests: year 636 (Yarmuk and Qadisiyya), label 632–750. Jerusalem 638 (not 637). Peak area hedged "11–15 million km²"
  (Umayyad article 11–15M; conquests article 13M).
- vikings-lindisfarne: 8 June 793 is the accepted date (the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle's text has January). Wikipedia's Viking Age
  lead gives c. 800–1050; 793–1066 is the conventional English framing. Vinland given as c. 1021 (tree-ring date), not "c. 1000".
- maya-collapse: year 800, label c. 750–950. Tikal's last monument 869 (Stela 11); the 909/910 Toniná date was not on fetched
  pages and is omitted. Overpopulation not listed by the source, so not named.
- house-of-wisdom: label "9th century CE"; its founding is uncertain (Harun al-Rashid or al-Mansur collections, public
  under al-Ma'mun). Gutas and İhsanoğlu's doubts that it was an academy are reflected. The Siege of Baghdad page says Baghdad's
  big libraries reopened within two years, so "destroyed in 1258" is not claimed in the sack entry.
- diamond-sutra: 11 May 868; "oldest dated printed book", not "oldest printed item". Bi Sheng's movable type 1039–1048 (Qingli era).
- polynesian-settlement: dates follow Wilmshurst et al. 2011 (Society Is. c. 1025–1120; rest of East Polynesia c. 1190–1290; NZ
  c. 1230–1282). Disputed: the Polynesians article (citing Heath 2017/Carson 2013) gives earlier dates (Hawaii by c. 900, Rapa
  Nui by c. 1000, NZ by c. 1200); History of New Zealand puts the main settlement at c. 1320–1350, after the Kaharoa eruption
  (c. 1314). The 1025–1290 short chronology is used as the most cited high-precision study. The sweet-potato/America contact is hedged.
- song-inventions: year 1040, label c. 1024–1088. Jiaozi nationalised 1023, first government series 1024; "first paper money"
  is tagged "who?" on Wikipedia, so hedged "usually regarded as". Shen Kuo's 1088 date is from the Compass article.
- cahokia: c. 1050–1350 (the chronology table says 1400). Population hedged 10,000–20,000 (estimates range 6,000–40,000).
- great-schism: 16 July 1054; the source notes 1054 is conventional. Humbert's document is called a "charter"/bull; the entry says "document".
- first-crusade: year 1096 (departure), label 1095–1099.
- angkor-wat: label c. 1113–1150 (Suryavarman II's reign; construction 1122–1150 per the History section).
- genghis-khan: 1206; peak Mongol area 23.5 million km² (1294, infobox). The Onon-source map point is approximate.
- great-zimbabwe: label c. 1220–1450 (kingdom from the 13th c.; decline from c. 1450). Stone building began in the 11th century and
  the site was occupied into the 16th–17th. Population (10,000–18,000) not stated in the entry.
- fall-of-acre: 18 May 1291. Tortosa, Atlit (August 1291) and Ruad (1302) were not on fetched pages; the entry says only that
  "remaining mainland strongholds were lost the same year" (Crusades article: "last mainland strongholds in 1291").
- mansa-musa: reign c. 1312–1337; caravan sizes and "18 tons of gold" flagged as rhetorical; al-Umari's gold-price claim kept, but
  Schultz argues the drop was within normal fluctuation. Djinguereber c. 1327; al-Sahili's role not stated (disputed).
- tenochtitlan: "c. 1325" (13 March 1325 is a 1925 choice). Triple Alliance: Azcapotzalco defeated 1428; the treaty itself is
  dated 1430 in the Aztec Empire article. Population "over 200,000" (estimates up to 400,000).
- black-death: year 1347 (Messina/Sicily, October), label 1346–1353. Caffa siege phases 1343–44 and 1345–47; the corpse-hurling
  story (de Mussis) is flagged as doubtful. Europe deaths 25–60% (the infobox's global 25–50M conflicts with 75–200M Eurasia;
  no global figure is given).
- zheng-he: 317 ships / almost 28,000 men (first voyage). Treasure-ship size not given (127 m claim widely doubted).
- inca-pachacuti: "c. 1438" (Rowe's chronology); other scholars propose 1400–1425 starts and radiocarbon suggests earlier.
- gutenberg-press: label c. 1440–1455; Bible finished 1454 or 1455; copies 160–185 (letter says 158 or 180). Strasbourg not mentioned.
- columbus: landfall island hedged as "probably" San Salvador.
- vasco-da-gama: arrival "May 1498". The Vasco da Gama article gives 20 May 1498 at Kappad; the "Portuguese discovery of the sea
  route to India" article gives 17 May at Kappakadavu. Survivors "about 55" (one source: 55 of 148).

## Unverifiable / disputed (softened or omitted)
- Teotihuacan population; Tikal population (10,000–90,000) not given as a number.
- "First" claims: Diamond Sutra (dated, not earliest print); jiaozi (first paper money, hedged); Brahmagupta ("earliest known text").
- Aksum as "first sub-Saharan coinage" (not claimed); Ge'ez still in liturgical use (not claimed, not on fetched pages).
- House of Wisdom as a formal academy (presented as debated).
- 476 as "end of antiquity" is presented as a convention ("often used").
- Taíno population figures (Hispaniola 500,000 → <500 by 1526) not used; the general 80–95% Americas figure is cited.
