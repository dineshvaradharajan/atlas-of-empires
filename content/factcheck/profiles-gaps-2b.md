# Fact-check: new profiles, batch 2b (steppe and Danube)

Profiles: northern-yuan, oirats, dzungar-khanate, tumed, khanate-of-sibir, shaybanids, avar-khaganate, scythians.
"Map" figures below were checked against data/clio/cliopatria_polities_only_v021.geojson (the polygons the map draws).

- [northern-yuan] rulers[Tümen Zasagt Khan].note: "Restored unity with the help of Altan Khan and other princes" → "Assembled the six tumens and issued a new law code; Altan Khan recognised his authority" — more exact; Altan validated his authority, he did not restore unity with Altan's help — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCmen_Zasagt_Khan
- [oirats] rulers[Batula].reign: "1399 – 1408" → "1399 – 1416" — Mahmud (Batula) led the Oirats until Arughtai killed him in 1416, after the Ming beat him in 1414 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Emperor%27s_campaigns_against_the_Mongols
- [oirats] rulers[Batula].note: "Early Oirat leader in the decades after the Yuan fell" → "Early Oirat leader; beaten by the Ming in 1414 and killed by the eastern Mongol Arughtai in 1416" — same source — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Emperor%27s_campaigns_against_the_Mongols

## Confirmed (writer's doubts)
- Northern Yuan peak: Taagepera 1997, Table 2D, Mongol-Yüan: 5 Mm² ±1.5 in 1368 ("China lost"). Confirmed in the eScholarship PDF.
- Scythia peak: Turchin 2009, Table 2: "-400 Scythia Europe 1.00". Confirmed in the PDF.
- Avar peak: 0.8M km² c. 600 (Taagepera 1979), per Wikipedia's List of largest empires. Taagepera 1997 says only "Avar — tabulated in T79". T79 itself was not seen.
- Map figures checked against the geojson: Northern Yuan 5.08M km² 1519 – 1546; Oirats 3.96M km² 1450 – 1467; Tümed 2.62M km² 1552 – 1581; Shaybanids 1.75M km² 1507 – 1511; Avars 1.02M km² 617 – 622.

## Unverifiable / disputed
- [tumed] end 1632: no source found that ties a Tümed submission to 1632. That year Hong Taiji's campaign drove Ligdan west from Köke Khota. Ligdan had taken the city from Boshugtu in 1627, and the Tümed were later organised as two Qing banners around Guihua. Kept as a reasonable end date. Boshugtu's reign (1608 – 1636) comes only from Wikipedia's navbox.
- [tumed] Guihua: the Hohhot article dates the Ming name to 1575; the Altan Khan article ties it to the 1571 settlement. Kept "from 1575".
- [avar-khaganate] end 822: this is the last Frankish mention (Pohl's "567–822"). The khaganate itself collapsed after the Ring fell in 795 – 796, and the eastern part fell to Krum c. 804. Kept 822; the summary gives both.
- [dzungar-khanate] end 1758 vs 1757: Wikipedia's infobox has 1758. Amursana died and resistance ended in 1757, while the killings ran into 1758. Kept 1758 ("by 1758").
- [dzungar-khanate] published 3.6M km²: Wikipedia ties this figure to 1650, when the khanate was far smaller than at its 1680s peak. The profile keeps peak.year 1688 and says so in the note.
- [dzungar-khanate] capital: Wikipedia's Erdeni Batur article contradicts itself, naming both Yining and a site near Tacheng. Hedged.
- [scythians] start -700 / end -200: these are the mainstream range (Ciscaucasian kingdom c. 750 – 700 BCE; Kamianka ends c. 200 BCE; Sarmatians conquer in the 3rd – 2nd century BCE). Kept.
- [shaybanids] Bukhara taken in 1501 (Wikipedia) vs 1499/1500 in some accounts. Kept 1501.
- [northern-yuan] Ligdan's accession: 1603 or 1604 (Wikipedia contradicts itself). Kept 1604.
- [khanate-of-sibir] Ibak's death: c. 1493 or c. 1495. Kept "about 1495".
- [oirats] Esen's death: 1454 or 1455. The text gives no year.
- Britannica returned 403 (Yermak), so Wikipedia was used throughout.
