# Fact-check log: Defining Moments (content/moments.json)

38 moments: the 34 on the brief plus 4 additions. All sources were fetched during writing.
Britannica, UNESCO (whc.unesco.org), the Met Heilbrunn Timeline, Smarthistory and the British
Museum refused automated fetches (HTTP 403/429), so none of them are cited. Sources are cited

## Dates adjusted from the brief
- farming: "c. 9500" → "c. 9500–8500 BCE". Wild cereals were being cultivated from about 9500 (PPNA, Jericho founded
  9500–9000), but fully domesticated founder crops appear c. 8500–7600 BCE (emmer at Çayönü 10,250–9550 BP). Year stays -9500.
  — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founder_crops, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Pottery_Neolithic_A
- catalhoyuk: c. 7500 kept as start; label "c. 7500–5600 BCE" (infobox gives 7100–5600). Population: older estimates of
  5,000–8,000 replaced by recent estimate of 600–800 in a typical year of the middle phase. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87atalh%C3%B6y%C3%BCk
- copper: "Balkans/Iran" → Balkans only (Belovode, c. 5000 BCE). No fetched source gave a date for Iranian smelting
  (Tal-i Iblis is not covered by the cited pages), so Iran is not named; "also" says early copper-working is documented in the Near East.
  Wikipedia's Belovode article locates the site on Mt Rudnik; the Radivojević paper places it in eastern Serbia near Petrovac na Mlavi,
  which is what the entry uses. — https://www.hmsjournal.org/index.php/home/article/view/108
- wheel-plough: c. 3500 kept. Earliest wagon image (Bronocice) is c. 3635–3370; oldest surviving wheel 3340–3030. The brief's
  implied pairing of wheel and plough at one date is loose: the ard is tied to the 4th-millennium "secondary products" shift, but the
  oldest dated ploughed field is Kalibangan, c. 2800 BCE. The entry says "in the same era", not a single date.
  — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plough
- writing: c. 3400–3200 kept; first unequivocal Uruk tablets are Uruk IV, from c. 3300 BCE; Abydos U-j labels c. 3400–3200.
- uruk: "c. 3200" → label "c. 3500–3100 BCE" (Late Uruk period); peak c. 3100. Year stays -3200. Area given as c. 250 ha
  (Late Uruk) rather than 5.5–6 km² (that figure is the later walled city). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruk_period
- egypt-unified: "c. 3100" → "c. 3150–3100 BCE". The Early Dynastic Period is dated from c. 3150; Narmer c. 3100 ± 150;
  unification began under predecessors (Ka, Iry-Hor) and was completed by Narmer. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmer
- bronze: "c. 3000" → label "c. 3300–3000 BCE" (Near Eastern EBA from 3300; "by 3000 BC across much of the Old World").
  Placed at the Kestel tin mine (used 3250–1800 BCE) because bronze has no single place of origin.
- great-pyramid: "c. 2560" → "c. 2600–2560 BCE". Wikipedia now gives c. 2600 BCE (one study 2613–2577); Khufu "early
  26th century BC". Year stays -2560.
- sargon-akkad: 2334 kept (middle chronology); the entry notes "first empire" is a debated label. Alternatives 2324–2285 and 2316–2277.
- horse-chariot: "c. 2000" → "c. 2200–1800 BCE". DOM2 horses arose c. 2200 BCE in the Volga–Don region (Librado et al. 2021,
  as cited on Wikipedia); spoked-wheel chariots in Sintashta graves probably before 2000 BCE. The Nature paper itself redirected
  to a login page, so it is cited through Wikipedia. Placed at Arkaim (coordinates verified); the Sintashta type site is c. 30 km away.
- hammurabi: "c. 1754" → "c. 1755–1750 BCE". Wikipedia says composed c. 1753, late in a reign of 1792–1750 (middle chronology);
  the Louvre dates the stele 1792–1750. Year stays -1754.
- vedas: "Rigveda c. 1500–1200" → "c. 1500–1000 BCE", which Wikipedia gives as the most likely composition range (wider proposals
  1900–1200); codification c. 1200–1000 (Witzel). Upanishads: earliest probably 7th–6th c. BCE (Olivelle), others 600–300 BCE (Flood).
  Year -1300 as the midpoint. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads
- bronze-age-collapse: c. 1200–1150 kept. Year set to -1190 (Ugarit destroyed c. 1192–1185) so it follows the Olmec entry.
  Noted that Millek found many supposed c. 1200 destructions misdated.
- iron: "c. 1200" → "c. 1200–1000 BCE", year -1150. Iron smelting appears c. 1300 BCE (Anatolia, Caucasus or SE Europe) and spread
  across the Mediterranean in the 12th–11th centuries. The Hittite-monopoly idea is no longer consensus. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age
- phoenician-alphabet: c. 1050 kept as the conventional date; the oldest securely dated inscriptions are 10th century (Ahiram c. 1000).
- olympic-games: 776 kept, labelled "traditional"; archaeology suggests major games began c. 700 BCE.
- lydian-coinage: "c. 600" → "late 7th century BCE", year -620. The Phanes electrum coin is dated 625–600; Wikipedia's Lydia
  article says estimates run from 700 to 550, most commonly near the start of Alyattes' reign. Croesus' pure gold/silver coins c. 550.
- axial-age: 6th–5th c. kept; Buddha's dates given as debated (c. 563–483 or c. 480–400); Confucius traditional 551–479; Laozi only
  "according to tradition". Jaspers' span is 8th–3rd c.
- ashoka-kalinga: "c. 261" → "c. 261–260 BCE" (8th regnal year; Kalinga War article c. 261, Ashoka article c. 260).
- euclid-alexandria: c. 300 kept for Euclid; the Library was probably founded under Ptolemy II (r. 285–246), so the entry
  says so rather than dating the Library to 300.
- silk-road: "c. 138–126" → "c. 138–114 BCE". Zhang Qian returned in 125 BCE, not 126 (the Han dynasty article gives 139–125);
  the Wusun journey (119–115) was his third mission, not his second. Silk Road dated "around 114 BCE" (lead) or "around 130 BCE".
- paper: c. 2nd century BCE kept; Fangmatan fragment 179–141 BCE; early paper used for wrapping and padding; Cai Lun's 105 CE
  work described as a refinement. Year -160.
- caesar-augustus, carthage-corinth, cyrus-persia, athenian-democracy, persian-wars, alexander, qin-unification, han-dynasty,
  birth-of-jesus, shang-oracle-bones, indus-cities: dates as on the brief, all confirmed.

## Additions (4)
- caral (c. 2600–2000 BCE): Caral–Supe/Norte Chico, the oldest known civilisation in the Americas; large-scale building from c. 3200,
  Caral inhabited c. 2600–2000. Year -2620 so it sorts before the Indus entry.
- olmec (c. 1200–900 BCE): San Lorenzo, the largest Mesoamerican city of its time; colossal heads; the "mother culture" label is described as debated.
- lapita (c. 1200–890 BCE): the push into Remote Oceania after 1200 BCE; first Polynesian landfall at Nukuleka, Tonga, dated by U/Th to
  2838 ± 8 BP (c. 890 BCE). Bismarck Lapita given as 1600–1200 BCE per Wikipedia (some recent work prefers c. 1350–1300).
- nok-iron (c. 750–550 BCE): Nok bloomeries by c. 550 BCE, possibly 750 or earlier; independent African invention presented as likely but debated.

## Unverifiable / softened
- Nukuleka coordinates: the PLoS paper gives only a description (NE lagoon entrance, Tongatapu), so [-175.10, -21.13] is approximate.
  Belovode [21.42, 44.33], Fangmatan [106.00, 34.35] and Kestel [34.90, 37.70] are also approximate (within ~20 km). The Punjab
  point for the Vedas and the Bodh Gaya point for the Axial Age are representative, not exact sites of origin.
- Library of Alexandria size: 40,000–700,000 scrolls; written as "between tens and hundreds of thousands".
- Indian punch-marked coinage: "by the 4th century BCE, possibly earlier" (c. 575 is an uncertain early estimate).
- Christianity is described as "one of the world's largest religions"; no fetched page was used for a superlative.
