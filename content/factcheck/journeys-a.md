# Journeys slice A: ancient empires (content/journeys/a-ancient.json)

There are 20 journeys, in chronological order of span start. The app sorts them the same way, so the slice order given in the brief was not kept. Chains were checked against `public/data/clio/index.json` and `content/corrections.json`. "Uncovered" means years inside the span where the chained realm has no area rows, so the map draws nothing for this journey in those years.

Britannica (403) and the Met Heilbrunn Timeline (429) refused automated fetches, so they are not cited. Sources are Wikipedia statements, plus World History Encyclopedia for basics where its page gave the dates. Bronze Age dates follow the middle chronology. Every summary hedges them with "c.".

- **sumer**: chain Sumerian City-States −3400→−2334, full coverage. Proto-cuneiform is dated by the Uruk IV phase (c. 3300). Event years for Gilgamesh/Enmebaragesi (−2700, legendary) and Lugalzagesi's unification (−2340, placed within his c. 2358–2334 reign) are approximate. The span ends at −2334 to follow Lugalzagesi's article. The Akkadian Empire and Sargon articles instead date his defeat by Sargon c. 2300 (Sallaberger & Schrakamp: 2292). That date is used in the Akkad journey.
- **akkadian-empire**: chain Akkadian Empire −2300→−2154. Cliopatria starts at −2300, so the founding years −2334…−2301 are uncovered. The span keeps Sargon's accession (c. 2334). The following are undated in the sources and are placed inside the right reign: Sargon's Mari campaign (−2290), Naram-Sin's Great Revolt and deification (−2240), and the Lullubi stele (c. 2250, from a caption).
- **ur-iii**: Cliopatria has no Ur III realm. The chain uses Sumerian City-States −2100→−2004, which in that slice is only a c. 12,600 km² polygon around Ur and Uruk. Cliopatria also draws a large "Gutian Dynasty" polygon in −2100…−2001, although the Gutians were expelled c. 2120. The map therefore badly understates the Ur III state (Taagepera: c. 0.1M km²). −2112…−2101 is uncovered. Shulgi's year-names are converted to BCE from his accession in 2094 (year 21 = −2074, year 25 = −2070, year 30 = −2065, year 37 = −2058, year 45 = −2050). Isin's break is dated by Ibbi-Sin's year 8 (−2021). The Code of Ur-Nammu is attributed to Ur-Nammu or Shulgi, so it is left out of the events.
- **old-babylonian-empire**: chain Babylonia −1800→−1595, full coverage. The span starts at Hammurabi's accession (−1792), not the dynasty's founding (c. 1894), because Babylon was a minor city-state before him. Larsa is dated 1763; the article also gives 1764. The Code is dated only "late in his reign" (shown as −1755). Ilum-ma-ili's Sealand revolt falls within Samsu-iluna's years 18–29 (shown as −1725). The sack of Babylon is shown at c. 1595 (middle chronology); proposed dates range from 1736 to 1499 BCE, and eclipse studies have suggested 1659.
- **hittite-empire**: chain Hittites −1600→−1180. −1650…−1601 is uncovered, because Cliopatria starts 50 years after Hattusili I. Reigns follow Bryce (2005) via the List of Hittite kings. The Hittites article gives Tudhaliya IV as 1245–1215. The Egyptian treaty is shown at −1259 (Ramesses II year 21) to match the Egypt journey; the Hittites article and World History Encyclopedia say c. 1258. The end is c. 1190 for Hattusa's abandonment, and the span runs to −1180. The Carchemish/Dahamunzu event is placed at Tutankhamun's death (−1323).
- **egypt-new-kingdom**: chain Seventeenth Dynasty of Egypt −1549→−1501, then New Kingdom of Egypt −1500→−1077. The 17th-dynasty polygon is the Theban kingdom only, so the map understates the reunified Egypt of c. 1530–1501. Reigns follow the conventional low chronology of the dynasty articles (Ahmose 1549–1524). The New Kingdom article's infobox, using radiocarbon dating, gives 1570–1544 for Ahmose's accession. Thutmose I's Euphrates campaign is undated within his reign and shown at −1500. The New Kingdom's end is shown at Ramesses XI's death (1077); the 21st Dynasty is dated from 1069.
- **neo-assyrian-empire**: chain Assyria −911→−901, then Neo-Assyrian Empire −900→−609, full coverage. Cliopatria's "Assyrian Egypt" (−650…−616) duplicates the whole Neo-Assyrian empire and is not used. Cliopatria keeps Egypt inside the empire until −616, though Psamtik I made Egypt independent in 656, so the map overstates −655…−616.
- **neo-babylonian-empire**: chain Babylonia −626→−601, then Neo-Babylonian Empire −600→−539. Babylonia has no rows for −630…−616, so −626…−616 is uncovered (15 of 26 years covered in stage 1). Jerusalem's destruction is shown at 587 (also given as 586). The Tayma return is c. 543.
- **achaemenid-empire**: chain Achaemenid Empire −550→−330, full coverage. Cyrus's reign is shown from −550; he was king of Persia from 559, as his reign name notes. Lydia is shown at 547 (also given as 546).
- **rome**: chain Roman Republic −500→−32, then Roman Empire −31→394, then Western Roman Empire 395→475. Uncovered years are −509…−501 (Cliopatria starts the Republic at −500) and 476. corrections.json has nothing for these realms. Dynasty years follow the List of Roman emperors.
- **alexander**: chain Macedonian Empire −359→−301. The span runs past Alexander's death to Ipsus (301), so the empire's breakup is shown. **Data gap:** for −331…−324 Cliopatria draws Alexander's conquests under the label "Ptolemaic Kingdom" (1.2M and 4.8M km²). corrections.json hides those polygons. The "Macedonian Empire" polygon for those years is only c. 218,000 km², so the map badly understates Alexander's empire at its height until it jumps to 4.7M km² at −323. Suggested fix: relabel Ptolemaic Kingdom −331…−324 as Alexander's empire (key "Macedonian Empire") instead of hiding it. Alexander IV's murder is shown at 309; estimates run from 311 to 304.
- **maurya-empire**: chain Maurya Empire −318→−185. −321…−319 is uncovered. Founding is given as c. 321–320 (World History Encyclopedia: 322/321). The Seleucid treaty is c. 302 per Wikipedia; World History Encyclopedia says 305. Kalinga is c. 261 (World History Encyclopedia: c. 260). The end is shown at 185; World History Encyclopedia also gives 187, and Wikipedia mentions 180.
- **seleucid-empire**: chain Seleucid Empire −312→−64. Cliopatria has no rows for −315…−308, so −312…−308 is uncovered. The gap at −77…−67 is genuine: Tigranes held Syria then. The span ends −63 for Pompey's annexation (Cliopatria ends −64; 64 is also often given). Panium is shown at 200, as in the Ptolemaic Kingdom article; the Antiochus III article says 198. The Bactria/Parthia secession is "mid-3rd century", shown at −250. The map peak (3.0M km²) is below Taagepera's 3.9M, as already noted in corrections.json.
- **ptolemaic-kingdom**: chain Ptolemaic Kingdom −305→−30. Coverage is full; the realm rows start at −307, and the hidden −331…−324 years are excluded. The span starts at the royal title (305), not the satrapy (323).
- **parthian-empire**: chain Parthian Empire −239→224. Cliopatria starts at −239, so −247…−240 is uncovered; corrections.json clips the realm to −247…224. The founding is "c. 247" (Arsacid era). Arsaces II–Phraates I is grouped as one reign; Phraates II and others are left out. Avidius Cassius's burning of Seleucia and Ctesiphon is dated 164 (often 165).
- **qin-han**: chain Qin −221→−219, then Qin Dynasty −218→−209, then Zhai −208→−207, then Han Dynasty −202→5, then Xin Dynasty 6→29, then Han Dynasty 30→220. Cliopatria labels the 1.63M km² Qin remnant of −208…−207 "Zhai", though its Wikipedia link is the Qin dynasty. −206…−203 is uncovered, the Chu–Han contention. Cliopatria's Xin runs 6–29, while Xin as a dynasty was 9–23 and the Han were restored in 25. corrections.json clips Han to 220. The map's Han peak (4.58M km²) is below Taagepera's 6.0–6.5M. The Qin conquest years are 230–221; the Qin article gives 226 for Yan, which other accounts date to 222.
- **xiongnu**: chain Xiongnu −208→91. The span ends with the Northern Xiongnu defeat in 91. Cliopatria continues a c. 212,000 km² remnant to 153, and the Southern Xiongnu dissolved c. 216. The map peak of 5.6M km² is far below Taagepera's c. 9M. Reign dates come from the Chanyu article and are partly uncertain. The end of Punu's reign is unknown, so Punu and Youliu are grouped for 46–87. Modu's defeat of the Yuezhi is dated 203 in his article.
- **kushan-empire**: chain Kushan Empire 43→237, then Eastern Kushans 238→346. Uncovered years are 30–42 and 347–375. Founding (c. 30) and end (c. 375) are both approximate. Kujula Kadphises's reign is variously 25–85, 30–80, 40–90 or 50–90 (30–80 used). Samudragupta's suzerainty is "mid-4th century", shown at 350. The Western Kushans realm (238–259) is not chained, because only one realm is drawn at a time.
- **sasanian-empire**: chain Sasanian Empire 224→643. Cliopatria ends at 643, so 644–651 is uncovered (noted in corrections.json; the end is Yazdegerd III's death in 651). Qadisiyyah is shown at 636; World History Encyclopedia says 637. The Shapur I Wikipedia article is flagged for possible LLM-generated text, so it is not cited.
- **gupta-empire**: chain Gupta Empire 324→550. 319–323 is uncovered. The conquest of the Western Satraps is dated only to between c. 397 and 409, shown at 405. Toramana's sack of Kausambi is c. 497–500. The outcome of Eran (510) is debated. The end is "mid-6th century": the last inscription is 542/543; World History Encyclopedia says c. 550, with metadata saying 575.

## Empty chains
None. Ur III has no realm of its own; it uses the Sumerian City-States polygon, which severely understates it (see above).

## Count
20 journeys written. All passed validation: JSON parses, ids are unique, chain names exist and lie inside the realm years, events and reigns are ascending and inside the span, and `tools/content.py` reports no journey problems.
