# Fact-check log: profiles-gaps-2a (steppe profiles: Xianbei, Rouran, Hephthalites, Türgesh, Uyghur Khaganate, Kimek–Kipchak, Kara-Khanids, Qara Khitai)

Method: every date, ruler, reign, capital, peak figure, battle and causal claim was checked against fetched pages. Encyclopaedia Iranica (iranicaonline.org) and eScholarship returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching. Wayback copies were not used to get round this. Claims were checked instead against English Wikipedia articles (their cited statements) and Wikipedia's "List of largest empires". The Turchin, Adams & Hall (2006) and Turchin (2009) PDFs were read directly from their archived copies. The Taagepera (1997) PDF text was extracted from the eScholarship file. Peak areas marked "as drawn on this map" were checked against the project's Cliopatria data (data/clio/realms.txt and cliopatria_polities_only_v021.geojson): Türgesh 4.04M km² (724–740), Kimek–Kipchak 3.02M km² (1085–1093), Kara-Khanids 1.31M km² (1040–1045). All three match.

## Unverifiable / disputed
- [xianbei] peak.year 177 / areaKm2 4.5M: Scheidel (2020, via Wikipedia) gives 4.5M km² for c. 200, by which time the confederation had already split up. The year 177 (Tanshihuai's height, his defeat of the Han army) was kept because the note says plainly that Scheidel's date is c. 200. The map draws 5.2M km² (161–164). Dates 156–235 are sound: 156 is Tanshihuai's first recorded raid, and 235 is Kebineng's assassination.
- [rouran-khaganate] start 402: correct for Shelun's unification and his khagan title. Wikipedia's infobox gives 330, counting from Mugulü's chiefdom.
- [hephthalites] start 450: Wikipedia says "a significant political entity in Bactria around 450 CE, or sometime before", and the infobox gives the 440s, so the profile's "by about 450" stands. Other scholars put them securely in Tokharistan only from c. 466–484. Kept.
- [hephthalites] final war: sources date it anywhere from 555 to 567. They include Bivar/Iranica (558–561), Sinor (557–561) and Baumer (557–563). Wikipedia places the decisive eight-day battle near Qarshi (Gol-Zarriun) "perhaps in 557". The profile's "Around 560 ... crushed them in Sogdiana" and end 560 (Cambridge World History) fit this range. Gatfar's "c. 560" is kept, although Wikipedia's ruler list dates Ghadfar c. 567–568 (Chavannes's dating).
- [turgesh] Suluk's election: c. 716 is kept (Suluk article: 715 or 716; reign 716–738). The Türgesh article says 714. His death: Suluk article "737 or 738"; Kharistan and Kül-chor articles "early in 738".
- [turgesh] peak 4.0M km² is the map's own Cliopatria figure. No Taagepera or Turchin figure exists.
- [uyghur-khaganate] "40 rolls of silk for each horse": in Wikipedia (Uyghur Khaganate) and Di Cosmo. Scholars debate whether this was a real price or a nominal one. Bögü's conversion is dated 762 in the Uyghur article and 763 in the Bögü Qaghan article; 762 is kept.
- [kimek-kipchak] dates 880–1241 join two phases. Wikipedia dates the Kimak confederation 880–1035 and says it fell in the mid-11th century. 1241 is Köten's death in Hungary; the Mongols had crossed the Volga in 1236 and broken the Cumans in 1237–1240. Kept as a reasonable span. The Kimek khagans' "c. 880 – 1050" fits "fall in the mid-11th century".
- [kara-khanids] start 840 follows Wikipedia's infobox and the Karluk timeline: Bilge Kul Qadir Khan proclaimed khagan when the Uyghur Khaganate fell. Some scholars put the dynasty's emergence later, in the late 9th century.
- [kara-khanids] legacy "still celebrated by the Uyghurs and in Kyrgyzstan": partly confirmed. Modern Uyghurs identify with the Karakhanids (Wikipedia), and Yusuf Balasaguni appears on the Kyrgyz 1000-som note.
- Iranica: both canonical Iranica URLs (hephthalites, ilak-khanids) were refused with 403. Every claim they back is also backed by a fetched source in the same profile.
