# Fact-check log: story "How the dinosaurs evolved"

File: `content/prehistory/stories/dinosaurs.json` (14 chapters, 251.9 to 66 million years ago).

## How sources were read

- Wikipedia articles were read in full through the Wikipedia API (plain-text extracts) and are cited by their normal article URL.
- Peer-reviewed papers are cited by their DOI link. Publisher pages, PubMed and PubMed Central refused automated fetches (403 or a bot check), so each paper's title, authors and abstract were read from its Europe PMC record (looked up by DOI). Only claims stated in those abstracts are attributed to the papers.
- Museum pages read directly: Natural History Museum London Dino Directory (Archaeopteryx, Plateosaurus, Eoraptor, Diplodocus, Stegosaurus, Corythosaurus, Tyrannosaurus, Confuciusornis) and its fossil-birds collection page; University of Birmingham news release on Teleocrater; Paul Sereno's University of Chicago page on Eoraptor; University of Washington news release on Asilisaurus (used for checking only).
- AMNH, UNESCO, Smithsonian Magazine, Royal Society Publishing, NPR and Lamont-Doherty pages could not be fetched (403, timeouts or certificate errors) and are not cited.

## Chapters and what each rests on

1. Survivors of the Great Dying (251.9 Ma, Karoo): 81 percent marine / 70 percent land-vertebrate species lost (Wikipedia, Permian-Triassic extinction event); Lystrosaurus the commonest Early Triassic land fossil; Proterosuchus and Prolacerta in the lower Lystrosaurus zone; Proterosuchus up to 3.5 m. Date agrees with e:end-permian-extinction (251.94).
2. The first dinosaur cousins (245 Ma, Manda Beds, Tanzania; Poland footprints 250 Ma): Teleocrater 2.1-3 m, four-legged, more than 245 million years old (Birmingham release; Nesbitt et al. 2017 abstract seen); Asilisaurus with leaf-shaped teeth, beak-like lower jaw, four-legged (Nesbitt et al. 2010 abstract); Nyasasaurus from a humerus and vertebrae, possibly the earliest dinosaur (Wikipedia); Prorotodactylus tracks 249-251 Ma (Brusatte et al. 2011 abstract).
3. The first dinosaurs (231 Ma, Ischigualasto): ages 231.7-225 Ma (Ischigualasto Formation) and 233.23 Ma at Cerro da Alemoa (Santa Maria Formation); Herrerasaurus the commonest carnivore; Eoraptor about 10 kg (NHM); Eodromaeus a basal theropod and Eoraptor reassessed as a basal sauropodomorph (Martinez et al. 2011). Agrees with e:ischigualasto (231) and o:dinosaurs (233).
4. Dinosaurs spread across Pangaea (210 Ma, Trossingen): Plateosaurus 214-204 Ma, 7 m (NHM), over 50 localities, Trossingen and Frick (1976) mass finds; Coelophysis quarry at Ghost Ranch dug 1947-48; Triassic land faunas dominated by phytosaurs and pseudosuchians (Wikipedia, Triassic-Jurassic extinction event).
5. Rivals vanish, dinosaurs take over (201.4 Ma, Newark Basin): CAMP as the generally accepted cause; all pseudosuchians except small crocodylomorphs and the phytosaurs gone; large theropods less than 10,000 years after the boundary and dinosaur dominance for 135 million years (Olsen et al. 2002); Eubrontes 25-50 cm, maker unknown, possibly Dilophosaurus-like; Rocky Hill tracks uncovered 1966, about 200 Ma. Agrees with e:end-triassic-extinction (201.4).
6. Feathered dinosaurs before the first bird (160 Ma, Jianchang, Liaoning): Anchiornis 160.89-160.25 Ma, hundreds of specimens, four-winged (Wikipedia); long feathers on the foot (Hu et al. 2009); grey and dark body, rufous crown, white limb feathers with black spangles (Li et al. 2010). Matches the claimMa 160 noted in o:birds.
7. Giants of the Late Jurassic (152 Ma, Morrison): formation 156.3-146.8 Ma, 1.5 million km2, Camarasaurus the commonest sauropod (Wikipedia); Diplodocus 26 m (NHM); Tendaguru fauna similar to the Morrison; Giraffatitan 150-145 Ma. Agrees with e:morrison-formation (152).
8. Archaeopteryx (150 Ma, Solnhofen): found two years after On the Origin of Species, 14 body fossils, teeth, three clawed fingers, long bony tail (Wikipedia); 0.5 m, flying or gliding unclear, London specimen bought 1862 (NHM). NHM gives 149-145 Ma; the story keeps 150 Ma to agree with e:solnhofen-limestone and Wikipedia.
9. Feathers, flowers and early birds (125 Ma, Yixian Formation): deposition 125.755-124.122 Ma (Wikipedia, 2021 dates); Sinosauropteryx first non-avialan dinosaur with feathers, countershaded with a banded tail; Confuciusornis crow-sized, toothless beak, several hundred skeletons; Archaefructus at least 124.6 Ma (Sun et al. 2002). Microraptor is from the slightly younger Jiufotang Formation (125-120 Ma) of the same Jehol Biota; it is listed as a species but not placed at the Yixian site. Agrees with e:jehol-biota (125).
10. Titans on a splitting supercontinent (101.6 Ma, La Flecha, Chubut): Patagotitan dated 101.62 Ma, at least six individuals, size revised from 37 m / 69 t to about 31 m / 50-60 t (Wikipedia); an endemic Patagonian clade holds most giant titanosaurs (Carballido et al. 2017 abstract); Argentinosaurus found 1987 about 8 km east of Plaza Huincul. The Huincul Formation is now given as Albian (Wikipedia, citing a 2026 study); older sources put it in the Cenomanian, so the site date (100.5 Ma) is approximate.
11. Horned and duck-billed dinosaurs flourish (75.5 Ma, Dinosaur Provincial Park): Dinosaur Park Formation 76.5-74.4 Ma on an alluvial to coastal plain beside the Bearpaw Sea; Centrosaurus and Corythosaurus dominate the lower part, Lambeosaurus and Styracosaurus the upper; 58 dinosaur species; UNESCO 1979.
12. Tyrannosaurus and the last dinosaurs (67 Ma, Hell Creek): Tyrannosaurus 12 m, 5,000-7,000 kg, 68-66 Ma (NHM); fauna, mild climate with palms, iridium boundary layer (Wikipedia); 32,000-year synchrony (Renne et al. 2013). Agrees with e:hell-creek-formation (67).
13. The asteroid strikes (66 Ma, Chicxulub): 10 km impactor, crater about 200 km (Wikipedia cites up to 300 km for the outer rim; the story keeps "about 200"), about 1 km of cover, found 1978 and identified 1991; three quarters of species lost; global ejecta layer (Schulte et al. 2010).
14. The dinosaurs that survived: birds (66 Ma, Eben-Emael): Asteriornis 66.8-66.7 Ma, CBR-Romontbos quarry, found 2000, galliform-like and anseriform-like features, co-occurring with an Ichthyornis-like bird (Field et al. 2020; Wikipedia); forest collapse and survival of non-tree-dwelling birds (Field et al. 2018). Chapter date set to 66 (the survival after the impact) although the fossil itself is 66.7 Ma, to stay inside the 252-66 range and after chapter 13.

## Disputed points (evidence set to "disputed")

- Chapter 2, age of the Manda Beds: traditionally Anisian (about 245 Ma, used by Nesbitt et al. 2010 and 2017); recent studies (Wikipedia, Manda Formation) suggest parts may be Ladinian or even earliest Carnian. Nyasasaurus: earliest dinosaur, or a close relative outside Dinosauria (Wikipedia). Prorotodactylus: Brusatte et al. 2011 read the tracks as dinosauromorph; others attribute them to euparkeriids, protorosaurs or pterosauromorphs (Wikipedia). Not in the text, logged here: Muller and Garcia (2020) proposed that silesaurs such as Asilisaurus are early ornithischians, not a sister group of dinosaurs (noted in that image's "note").
- Chapter 3, the dinosaur family tree: traditional Saurischia/Ornithischia split versus Ornithoscelida (theropods + ornithischians) of Baron, Norman and Barrett (2017); a November 2017 reanalysis by Langer and colleagues recovered the traditional arrangement; some analyses also give Phytodinosauria (Wikipedia, Ornithoscelida). Eoraptor's place: theropod (Sereno 1993; still listed so on Sereno's university page and the NHM Dino Directory) or basal sauropodomorph (Martinez et al. 2011; Wikipedia). The story follows the newer view and hedges with "usually".
- Chapter 6, whether Anchiornis is a bird: an early bird in some trees, a troodontid or other paravian in others (Hu et al. 2009 called it a troodontid; NHM says Anchiornis and Aurornis may be closer to true birds than Archaeopteryx).
- Chapter 11, whether dinosaurs were in decline before the impact: Sakamoto, Benton and Venditti (2016) found speciation falling behind extinction for tens of millions of years, except in hadrosauriforms and ceratopsids; Bonsor et al. (2020) found no strong support for a downturn. Matches e:were-dinosaurs-declining.
- Chapter 13, the role of the Deccan Traps: Schulte et al. (2010) and Chiarenza et al. (2020) conclude the impact was the main driver, Chiarenza finding volcanic warming may even have softened the impact winter; others argue the eruptions contributed substantially (Wikipedia, K-Pg extinction event; e:deccan-traps). Text keeps the mainstream view with "most researchers".
- Chapter 14, earliest modern birds: Vegavis was debated for over twenty years; a skull described in 2025 supported it as an anseriform (Wikipedia, Vegavis). Gene-clock dates for modern birds are older than any fossil (as noted in o:modern-birds); not re-verified here, so only stated generally.

## Not disputed but worth noting

- Chapter 5: Olsen et al. (2002) linked the end-Triassic turnover to an iridium anomaly and suggested an asteroid impact. That cause is no longer the mainstream view; Wikipedia (Triassic-Jurassic extinction event) gives CAMP volcanism as the generally accepted cause. The story uses Olsen et al. only for the footprint timing.
- Chapter 4: the NHM Dino Directory says Plateosaurus could move on two or four legs; Wikipedia describes it as bipedal. The text says it "walked on its hind legs" (Wikipedia); the NHM figure of 4,000 kg was left out to keep the sentence short.
- Chapter 12: earlier draft said dinosaur bones "stop at" the boundary; the fetched Hell Creek page only says the iridium boundary lies above (occasionally within) the formation, so the wording was softened.

## Left out, and why

- Early and Middle Jurassic (about 200-165 Ma): early sauropods, the first armoured dinosaurs (Scelidosaurus) and Gondwana's first break-up were left out to stay within 14 chapters; the gap is bridged in chapters 5 and 6.
- Spinosaurus and the Kem Kem beds, Cretaceous Asia (Gobi), polar dinosaurs and dinosaur eggs and nests: dropped for length.
- The Carnian Pluvial Episode is referenced (e:carnian-pluvial-episode) inside chapter 3 rather than given its own chapter.
- The impact hypothesis for the end-Triassic (see above).
- Gene-clock ages for modern birds (not verified on a fetched page in this pass).

## Source check, 2026-11-22

Every source cited in the 14 chapters was fetched again (Wikipedia through its API as plain text, papers through their Europe PMC records by DOI, NHM and University of Birmingham pages directly) and every claim in title, dateLabel, text, species, site notes and evidenceNote was checked against them. Each chapter's "ma" matches its dateLabel; no "ma" was changed.

Corrections:
- [the-first-dinosaurs] text: "about 233 to 231 million years old, a few million years after a spell of very wet climate" → "about 233 to 231 million years old, from during or soon after a spell of very wet climate" — the Carnian Pluvial Episode is dated to about 234-232 Ma, so the 233.2 Ma Brazilian dinosaurs fall within it and only the Ischigualasto beds come a few million years after it — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnian_pluvial_episode ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Formation ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischigualasto_Formation
- [the-first-dinosaurs] evidenceNote: "and a reanalysis later that year favoured the usual tree again." → "and a reanalysis later that year favoured the usual tree only weakly, so the question is still open." — the November 2017 reanalysis gave the traditional tree only weak support, not significantly better than Ornithoscelida, and Baron and colleagues' reply favoured Ornithoscelida again — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithoscelida
- [spreading-across-pangaea] text: "a long-necked plant-eater about 7 metres long" → "a long-necked plant-eater 5 to 10 metres long" — adults varied a lot in size, 4.8 to 10 m (developmental plasticity); 7 m (NHM) is only a typical figure — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateosaurus
- [spreading-across-pangaea] text: "have yielded many complete skeletons, and the finds hint that it may have lived in herds." → "have yielded many complete skeletons, probably of animals that sank into soft mud and were trapped." — the herd idea is outdated: the current reading of the Trossingen, Halberstadt and Frick bone beds is animals mired in mud, with "no indication of herding" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateosaurus
- [archaeopteryx] text: "and fourteen skeletons are now known." → "and fourteen fossil skeletons, some very incomplete, are now known." — the 14 are body fossils, some only fragments (for example the Karlsruhe specimen published in 2025, a forelimb and shoulder); the count includes the Chicago specimen described in 2025 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeopteryx
- [archaeopteryx] evidenceNote: "Fourteen skeletons, many with clear feather impressions," → "Fourteen fossil skeletons, most with feather impressions," — same reason; Wikipedia: "Most of these 14 fossils include impressions of feathers" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeopteryx
- [feathers-flowers-and-birds] text: "was the first dinosaur outside the bird line found with feathers" → "was the first dinosaur outside the birds and their closest relatives found with feathers" — the source says outside Avialae; in chapter 2 "bird line" means the whole bird branch of archosaurs, which includes Sinosauropteryx, so the old wording contradicted itself — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinosauropteryx
- [horns-and-duck-bills] text: "The badlands of Dinosaur Provincial Park were laid down" → "The main fossil beds of Dinosaur Provincial Park were laid down" — the park's badlands take in three formations; the 76.5-74.4 Ma dates belong to the Dinosaur Park Formation only — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Provincial_Park ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Park_Formation
- [horns-and-duck-bills] text: "showing how fast these plant-eaters evolved." → "showing how quickly the plant-eating fauna changed." — the source records faunal replacement up the section, not how fast the animals evolved (newcomers may have moved in from elsewhere) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Park_Formation
- [the-last-dinosaurs] text: "a metal rare on Earth but common in asteroids" → "a metal rare in Earth's crust but more common in asteroids" — iridium is rare in the crust, not in the Earth as a whole; Wikipedia: "more common in asteroids than in the Earth's crust" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous–Paleogene_extinction_event
- [the-birds-survive] text: "a small, long-legged shore bird with features" → "a small, long-legged bird that probably lived near the shore, with features" — Field et al. 2020 call its shoreline lifestyle only "possible"; "shore bird" could also be read as the modern shorebird group (Charadriiformes) — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2096-0 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteriornis
- [the-birds-survive] evidenceNote: "supported it as a waterfowl relative, and gene studies" → "supported it as a waterfowl relative, though some still doubt it, and most gene studies" — Crane et al. (2025) found only weak support and questioned whether the skull is a modern-type bird at all; the gene-clock claim is now sourced ("most molecular dating studies suggest a Cretaceous evolutionary radiation") — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegavis ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird
