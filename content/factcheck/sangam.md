# Fact-check: Tamilakam in the Sangam age (`content/supplements/sangam.json`)

This supplement replaces Cliopatria's three static blocks ("Early Cholas", "Early Pandyas", "Early Cheras",
291 BCE–305 CE). It has 23 time-bounded entries over 16 distinct areas: 3 crowned kingdoms, 9 Velir or
independent chiefdoms, and Kongu, Tondai Nadu, Aruva Nadu and Kuttanad. There are also 14 sites.
All rings are closed and clockwise, with 12–40 vertices, inside lon 74–81 and lat 8–14.5. They are built
from one shared-node lattice, so neighbours share identical edges. A grid test found no overlaps among
entries that are active at the same time, and 24 checkpoints (capitals, ports, digs) each fall in exactly
one region in −250, 50, 160 and 250.

## Ground rules

- **No ancient source draws a frontier.** Every line is inferred from where named places, rivers and hills
  sit. The cores (Kaveri delta, Vaigai–Tamirabarani, Malabar coast) are secure; the borders between them
  are not.
- **Chronology.** The splits over time follow K. A. Nilakanta Sastri's synchronisms, *A History of
  South India*, 1958, pp. 111–122. He fixes Senguttuvan to c. 173–195 CE through Gajabahu I of Sri Lanka,
  which puts the Sangam age at c. 100–250 CE: Udiyanjeral c. 130, Senguttuvan c. 180, Karikala and
  Perunjeral Irumporai c. 190, Nedunjeliyan of Talaiyalanganam c. 210. Other scholars date earlier:
  - Zvelebil prefers 100 BCE–250 CE (via Wikipedia, *Sangam period*).
  - Kanakasabhai (1904) puts Arisil Kilar at 110–140 CE.
  - The Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions point to the 1st century CE for Adiyaman Anji (Jambai) and the
    1st–2nd century CE for the Irumporai (Pugalur).
  - So the split years 150, 170 and 190 are **conventions within one chronology, not facts**.
- Sastri himself says the anthologies cover only "four or five continuous generations at the most" (p. 111).
  The time slices therefore represent the sequence of events more than absolute dates.

## Open questions (flagged in the notes)

1. **Where was Vanji, the Chera capital?** Three candidates:
   - Karur in Kongu (Sastri: the Vani river, Roman coins, Ptolemy's Karoura).
   - Tiruvanchikulam near Kodungallur (the commentators' view; Sastri calls the arguments for it "few tangible reasons").
   - Tiru-Karur on the Periyar near Kothamangalam (Kanakasabhai, who thought Caldwell's Karur identification a "mistake").
   The JSON lists all three.
2. **Sangam chronology.** See above. Karikala alone has been placed anywhere from the 1st century BCE to c. 190 CE.
3. **Keezhadi dating.** Amarnath Ramakrishna's report (submitted January 2023) proposes 8th c. BCE–3rd c. CE. In May 2025 the ASI asked for "concrete justification" of the 8th–5th c. BCE phase and for layer-wise documentation (Scroll, 23 May 2025). Wikipedia's *Keezhadi* gives 6th c. BCE radiocarbon dates. It also reports doubts (B. Basak, E. Harsha Vardhan) about whether the inscribed sherds come from the dated layers. The site is described as "contested".
4. **Pattanam = Muziris?** Likely, but Nagaswamy, Panikkar and Narayanan asked for more analysis. Gurukkal thought it might be a merchant quarter rather than the main city (Wikipedia *Muziris*).
5. **Who held Kuttanad and the cape?** The Periplus gives Nelcynda and the coast from Comari to the Pandyas. Ptolemy gives Melkynda and Komaria to the Aioi. Shown as a time change, but it may only be a difference between sources.
6. **The Jambai inscription's authenticity** was questioned soon after its discovery in 1981; R. Nagaswamy defended it (Madras Musings, via search results). Its 1st-century CE date conflicts with Sastri's c. 190 CE for Anji.
7. **Kodumanal dates** are reported uncalibrated and vary from source to source (5th–1st c. BCE is the common range). No calibrated publication was opened.
8. **Two Nannans** (Ezhimalai/Pali and Chenkanma). Sources disagree on which ruler held which land.
9. **Irungovel and Pekan** locations rest on tradition and later identifications.

## Map conflicts found (for whoever wires this in)

- **Cliopatria's "Maurya Empire" (−291 to −226) covers most of Tamil Nadu and Kerala.** It overlaps
  10 of the regions here, including the Chola, Chera and Tondai areas. That contradicts Ashoka's own Rock Edicts II and XIII,
  which name the Cholas, Pandyas, Satiyaputa and Keralaputa as peoples beyond his frontier. Sastri (p. 84)
  puts the southernmost Ashokan edicts in the Raichur, Chitaldrug and Kurnool districts, and thinks only "a part of the Tondaimandalam"
  might have been Mauryan. The Maurya polygon should be clipped to about 14° N or north of it when this supplement is shown.
- **Cliopatria's "Late Pallava Empire" (283–305)** overlaps Tondai Nadu for 283–300. Either end Tondai Nadu at 283
  or accept the overlap. The start of Pallava rule at Kanchi is itself uncertain.
