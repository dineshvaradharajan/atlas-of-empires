# sa-fixes: making the India checklist true

Layer `content/supplements/sa-fixes.json` (id `sa-fixes`), with `content/realm-aliases.json` and `content/india-verdicts.json`.
Input: the "Missing" and "Partly drawn" entries of `content/factcheck/india-dynasties.md` (tools/india_coverage.py), plus the six
"modern units" entries, which needed only aliases. Every entry got one of:

- **(a) alias**: the map draws it under another name (a Cliopatria realm, a reconstruction-layer region, a princely-states group, or a
  name produced by a relabel in content/corrections.json). Written to `content/realm-aliases.json`.
- **(b) no drawing needed**: vassal years inside a drawn overlord, Wikidata dates that differ from the mainstream, Cliopatria's
  time step, a city, a generic term, a modern unit, or a polity outside South Asia. Written to `content/india-verdicts.json`.
  Many entries carry both an alias and a note explaining the years the alias does not cover.
- **(c) real gap, drawn**: the state ruled land the map leaves empty. Drawn in `sa-fixes.json`.
- **gap left**: a real but small gap left undrawn (a petty state with no recorded frontier, or land Cliopatria's loose outlines
  already give to another realm, where a layer would overlap them). The verdict says so; listed below for later work.

## How the regions were made

- **Late Vedic kingdoms and early Mahajanapadas (1200–601 BCE).** Cliopatria's Mahajanapada outlines start in 600 BCE and are small
  discs, one of them (Gandhara) misplaced in the Poonch hills. The sa-fixes regions are hand-drawn core areas around the attested
  capitals, bounded by the rivers named in the sources (Ganges and Yamuna for Kuru and Panchala, Gomti and Gandak for Kosala, Son and
  Ganges for Magadha), checked for overlaps on a rendered basemap. Name and key are Cliopatria's (`Janapada of Kuru` etc.), so the
  colours join its outlines in 600 BCE. Start dates: Kuru 1200 BCE (Wikipedia: c. 1200–900), Panchala 1100 BCE (Wikipedia: c. 1100),
  Kashi 1000 BCE (Wikidata; Wikipedia's 1375 BCE rests on epic tradition), the others 700 BCE (Wikipedia gives c. 700 for Vatsa and
  Surasena, c. 7th century for Kosala). Gandhara is cut back where Cliopatria's Median Kingdom outline reaches the Indus (615–601 BCE).
  Vajji was not drawn before 600 BCE (Wikipedia dates the league to the 6th century).
- **Anuradhapura, 377–292 BCE.** The Rajarata dry zone until Cliopatria's island-wide outline begins in 291 BCE.
- **1551–1578 in the west.** The medieval layer stops in 1550 and Cliopatria's Mughal outline reaches Gujarat, Khandesh and Malwa only
  in 1572, Bikaner in 1564 and Marwar and Jaisalmer in 1579, leaving these lands empty. The last slices of the sa-medieval regions
  (Gujarat 1536–50, Khandesh, Malwa 1519–30, Marwar 1545–50, Jaisalmer, Bikaner) were carried forward to the year before the Mughal
  outline arrives, and cut back by raster subtraction (0.04° grid, traced and simplified to ~0.035°) wherever Cliopatria's Sur, Mughal,
  Berar or Ahmadnagar outlines or another layer already draw something.
- **Sirohi (1352–1571).** The princely-states layer's 1909 outline (Imperial Gazetteer and OpenHistoricalMap), cut back against
  Maldeo's Marwar (1532–43) and sa-earlymodern's Mewar (1550–71). Before 1352 Cliopatria's Khalji and Tughluq outlines cover the area.
- **Garhwal (1358–1539).** The sa-earlymodern 1550 outline carried back, cut against the medieval layer's Guge.
- Checks: no two sa-fixes regions overlap in the same years, no region overlaps another layer's region or a Cliopatria realm in the
  same years (grid test at 0.1°/0.04°), no self-intersections. The Gujarat outline keeps the sa-medieval shape, which touches
  Cliopatria's tiny Portuguese enclaves (Diu, Daman) by a few cells, as the medieval layer already did.

## Gaps left, for later work

- **Rajput and Gujarat chiefdoms in the 14th–16th centuries**: Amber (1408–1528), Bundi (1352–1507, 1542–67), Karauli (1354–1530),
  Idar (1353–1571), Dungarpur/Vagad and Banswara (1353–1571), Palanpur, Rajpipla, Lunavada, Alirajpur, Barwani, Orchha (1501–30). The
  medieval layer judged them too small or too poorly bounded; their 1909 borders would overstate their medieval extent.
- **Odisha and Chhattisgarh hill states** (Patna, Kalahandi, Sonepur, Bamra, Koriya) between the Gajapati fall and the Mughal outline
  (c. 1355–1660 in the west): the Chauhan kingdom of Patna/Sambalpur deserves a region if a dated source for its extent is found.
- **Punjab hill states 1773–1821** (Kangra under Sansar Chand, Mandi, Suket, Kahlur, Baghal, Chamba, Bashahr): blank between the Durrani
  and Sikh/British outlines.
- **Lumped by Cliopatria** (a layer would overlap its outlines): Chitral 1571–1884, Bahawalpur 1690–1857, Bhopal 1723–1857, Guge
  1551–1630, the Bengal Sur and Karrani sultans under the 'Sur Empire' outline 1554–76, Shashanka's Gauda 612–625 under Harsha's
  Empire. These need corrections.json relabels rather than layers.
- **Sirohi 1752–1857** sits inside sa-earlymodern's 'Kingdom of Marwar (Jodhpur)' outline, which takes in a state Jodhpur never ruled.

## Uncertainties

1. Vedic extents are conventions around capitals; only the order of magnitude and the river frontiers are meant. The start dates
   1200/1100/1000/700 BCE follow Wikipedia and Wikidata, not any dated political event.
2. Garhwal's unified extent from 1358 is uncertain: the union of the hill forts is credited to Ajay Pal, whose reign is put in the 14th
   or the late 15th century.
3. Jaisalmer is drawn to 1578 although it accepted Akbar's overlordship in 1570 (kept as an autonomous tributary until the Mughal outline
   arrives); Marwar stops in 1563 because Jodhpur itself was in Mughal hands from 1564.
4. Princely-state verdicts were written from a template: the realms listed are those whose outlines cover the state's Wikidata point
   (corrected by hand for Sitamau, Bhavnagar, Keonjhar, Jind, Faridkot, Narsinghgarh and Patiala, whose Wikidata coordinates are wrong).
   Founding years before 1500 are the ruling houses' traditional ones.
