# Storyboard: Russian invasion of Ukraine (`content/cinema/russian-invasion-of-ukraine.json`)

Sides: **0 = Ukraine, 1 = Russia and North Korea**. Span 27 Feb 2014 – 31 Dec 2024. **The war continues**: the storyboard ends with the map data at 31 Dec 2024 and the last chapter says so.

## Chapters and why
1. **Crimea and Donbas, 2014–2015** (context): seizure and annexation of Crimea, Sloviansk, Ilovaisk and Novoazovsk, Debaltseve.
2. **The invasion and the drive on Kyiv** (24 Feb – 28 Mar 2022).
3. **Russia withdraws from the north** (28 Mar – 12 Apr 2022), Bucha.
4. **The land bridge and Mariupol** (24 Feb – 20 May 2022).
5. **The battle for Donbas and the Kharkiv counteroffensive** (Apr – Oct 2022).
6. **Kherson retaken** (29 Aug – 12 Nov 2022).
7. **Bakhmut and the 2023 counteroffensive** (May – Nov 2023).
8. **Avdiivka, the push on Pokrovsk and the Kursk incursion** (Oct 2023 – Dec 2024).

## Maps traced
- **2022–2024:** 52 dated revisions of the ISW-based Commons map *File:2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.svg* (24 Feb 2022 … 31 Dec 2024), fetched as 1920-px thumbnails (the originals were rate-limited). The map frame is constant across revisions; it was georeferenced with a quadratic fit on 29 city circles from the SVG (residuals ≤ 3.5 km). Each thumbnail was sampled on a 0.02° grid: occupied pink → Russian-held; yellow / light blue (retaken) → Ukrainian-held; labels, arrows and lines take the majority of the surrounding known cells. Result clipped to Ukraine (geoBoundaries ADM1), smoothed, extended a little over the sea, and split by oblast groups (Crimea; Kherson; Zaporizhzhia; Donetsk; Luhansk+Kharkiv; the north) so each outline keeps enough points. Areas: 49.5k km² on 24 Feb 2022, peak ~159k km² (21 Mar 2022, as that map drew the north), ~102k after Kherson, ~106.5k at the end of 2024 — consistent with published estimates.
- **Kursk:** the Ukrainian-held part of Kursk region (light-blue on Russian soil in the same map), side 0, from 6 Aug 2024.
- **2014–2015:** hand-drawn rings anchored on towns, from revisions of *File:Map of the war in Donbass.svg* (12 Aug and 6 Sep 2014) plus the 24 Feb 2022 map for the line of contact from Feb 2015. Mid-June and late-July 2014 rings are approximate.

## Base-map gaps fixed
- No Russian-held Ukraine after 2022 in the base map: all occupied areas are side-1 occupation areas.
- Crimea overlaps Ukraine's polygon until 2023: covered by the "Crimea (held by Russia since 2014)" area.
- Side membership starts in 2022, so 2014–15 places can only be tested as Russian-held (claims are all holder 1 there).

## Uncertainties
- Accuracy is a few kilometres (thumbnail resolution, outline simplification and 192-point resampling in the app). Two claim points were nudged into the drawn area: Mariupol (northern districts, 37.52°E 47.13°N) and Kherson (northern districts, 32.60°E 46.67°N, dated 15 Nov when the source map caught up).
- In the north in March 2022 the source map coloured broad zones; Russian forces actually held mainly roads and villages, which the chapter text says. A sourced Ukrainian-held claim (Shostka, from the Commons revision) explains the large gap between the Chernihiv and Sumy axes.
- Military casualties are disputed; battles give ranges or say "figures disputed". Civilian deaths in Mariupol are the UN's confirmed 1,348 with its note that the real number is thousands higher.
- Neutral wording: "held by Russia and Russian-backed forces"; annexations are described as rejected by almost all countries.

## Warnings left (0 errors, 3 warnings)
- Arrow to Berdiansk ends on a coast that the coarse base map draws as sea.
- "Little visible change" in the 2023 chapter: the counteroffensive's real gains were only a few hundred km² (stated in the text).
- Pocket of 1,347 km² at 33.14°E 50.94°N on 10 Mar 2022 (between the Konotop and Bakhmach–Nizhyn zones): Ukrainian-held ground in the source map; under the 2,500 km² error threshold.
