## Unverifiable / disputed

- [1815] "Qing China ... still the world's largest economy" and "a third of humanity": standard Maddison-based estimates (c. 1/3 of world GDP and population c. 1820); not changed, but no fetched page states the figure directly.
- [1815] Dutch "loose claim" to western New Guinea: formal Dutch claim dates from 1828 (via the Sultan of Tidore's older claims); left as is because it says "loosely".
- [1900, 1945] British Empire share of world land ("about a fifth" in 1900; "still about a fifth" in 1945): no year-specific Taagepera figure found for those years; plausible next to 24.5M km² (1880) and 35.5M km² (1920 peak), so left unchanged.
- [1945] Sétif massacre "thousands of Algerians": kept; historians' estimates range from 3,000 to 30,000 (French official count 1,020; Algerian government 45,000).
- [1945] "The US emerges with half the world's industrial output": a widely repeated round figure; not changed.
- [1945] Ibn Saud–Roosevelt meeting "beginning the US-Saudi oil partnership": kept; Brookings describes it as the origin of the security-for-oil bargain, though the oil concession itself dates to 1933.
- [1938] Gulag "over a million prisoners" and Great Purge "hundreds of thousands" killed: consistent with mainstream figures; not separately fetched.
- [1994] US share of world output ("about a quarter") and [2000] ("about 30 percent"): consistent with nominal-GDP estimates; not separately fetched.
- [1920] Trianon "two-thirds of its land": source gives 72 percent; wording kept.
