# Fact-check: new empire profiles, batch 1 (profiles-gaps-1)

Input: scratchpad `profiles/out-1.json` (13 profiles: Rashidun, Samanid, Saffarid, Buyid, Hamdanid, Ghaznavid,
Ghurid, Khwarazmian, Zengid, Ayyubid, Aq Qoyunlu, Hotak, Median).

Peak-area figures were checked against the text of Taagepera (1997) Appendix (escholarship PDF) and Turchin, Adams &
Hall (2006) Table 1 (JWSR PDF), and against Wikipedia's "List of largest empires". All seven claimed figures are
correct: Rashidun/Caliphate 6.4M 655 (T97), Samanid 2.85M 928 (T97 and TAH06), Buyid 1.6M 980 (T97 and TAH06),
Ghaznavid 3.4M 1029 (T97 and TAH06), Khwarazm 3.6M 1218 (T97) / 2.3M 1210 (TAH06), Ayyubid 2.0M 1190 (TAH06) /
1.7M 1200 (T97), Media 2.8M 585 BCE (TAH06; also Taagepera 1979 per the Wikipedia list).

## Unverifiable / disputed

- [ghurid-dynasty] start 1149: Wikipedia dates the dynasty to the 8th/9th century (as local chiefs). 1149, the
  accession of Ala al-Din Husayn (reign 1149–1161, confirmed), is kept as the start of Ghur as an independent
  power, and the summary already notes the earlier generations. Rulers and reigns were confirmed: Ghiyath al-Din
  1163–1203; Mu'izz al-Din at Ghazni from 1173 and sole sultan 1203–1206. Sayf al-Din Muhammad (1161–1163) is
  left out, which is acceptable for a selection. Wikipedia's list gives 2.0M km² for 1200 (another source); the
  profile's 2.1M is the map's own figure.
- [saffarid-dynasty] end 1002: the Saffarid infobox and text give 1002, but the page's navigation boxes say 1003
  (AH 393 spans 1002–03). Kept 1002. Amr's reign of 879–901 matches Wikipedia; his capture in 900 near Balkh and
  execution in 902 are confirmed.
- [hamdanid-dynasty] end 1004: matches the infobox; the article text says the Fatimids deposed the Hamdanids in 1003.
  Kept 1004.
- [aq-qoyunlu] end 1508: matches the Wikipedia infobox (1378–1508). The Safavids won in 1501 and conquered Fars,
  Kerman and Iraq-Ajami by 1503, Diyarbakır in 1507–08 and Mesopotamia in autumn 1508. The summary's
  "1501 … last lands fell by 1508" is accurate.
- [ghaznavid-empire] Mas'ud I's death: Wikipedia's Mas'ud I infobox says 17 January 1040, while the Ghaznavids
  list ends his reign in 1041 and Iranica (403, not readable) is usually cited for 1041. The reign now ends at his
  deposition in 1040 and gives no death year.
- [median-kingdom] chronology: four competing chronologies exist (Herodotus 700–647/647–625; Rawlinson/Diakonoff
  700–678/678–625; Kashtariti 728–675/675–653; Grantovski). The profile keeps the Diakonoff-style dates that go
  with start −678 and flags Herodotus' figures in the notes. Cyaxares 625–585 and Astyages 585–550 are common to
  all of them.
- [rashidun-caliphate] Taagepera's 6.4M km² for 655 is for the "Islamic Caliphate" series as a whole (6.7M in 661);
  attributing it to the Rashidun in 655 matches Wikipedia's list.
- [zengid-dynasty] "about twenty madrasas": Wikipedia says 42 madrasas were built in Syria under Nur al-Din, and
  he personally sponsored half of them. Kept.
- [samanid-empire] Saman Khuda's Zoroastrian origin and conversion are as Wikipedia gives them ("originally a
  Zoroastrian"); the tradition is partly legendary.
