# War briefs, slice A

Wars: peloponnesian-war, second-punic-war, first-crusade, third-crusade, mongol-conquests, hundred-years-war.
Files: `content/war-briefs/<war-id>.json`. All six validated with `json.load`; side counts match `content/wars`, and every strategy `chapter` is a real id in `content/cinema/<war-id>.json`.

Note on sources: britannica.com (including its /summary and /print pages), worldhistory.org and historytoday.com all returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so none of them is cited. In their place: primary texts in scholarly editions (Thucydides, Plutarch, Polybius), Livius.org, a peer-reviewed article (Timothy May, *World History Connected*), and English Wikipedia, used only for figures it cites itself.

## Figures chosen
- **peloponnesian-war**: Athens c. 29,000 hoplites (13,000 field army plus 16,000 garrison; 300 triremes), from Thucydides 2.13 (https://web.viu.ca/johnstoi/thucydides/thucydides_2.htm). Sparta and allies "perhaps 60,000" hoplites invading Attica in 431 BCE, from Plutarch, *Pericles* 33 (https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Lives/Pericles*.html). Decelea: up to 20,000 slaves fled (Wikipedia, citing Thucydides). Aegospotami: Lysander, Cyrus's money (Wikipedia, Livius). Winner: Sparta (index 1).
- **second-punic-war**: both sides counted at Cannae, 216 BCE: Rome c. 86,000 (Polybius 3.113: about 80,000 foot and a little over 6,000 horse) and Carthage c. 50,000 (Polybius 3.114: a little over 40,000 foot and about 10,000 horse), https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/3*.html. Wikipedia's Cannae infobox gives 86,400 and 50,000. 22 legions by 213 BCE; peace terms of 10,000 talents over 50 years and ten warships (Wikipedia, Second Punic War). Spanish provinces 197 BCE (Wikipedia, Hispania). Winner: Rome (0).
- **first-crusade**: crusaders c. 60,000–100,000 who set out, counting non-combatants (Wikipedia gives 60,000–100,000 with non-combatants; its infobox says 130,000–160,000). There is no credible overall figure for the Seljuk or Fatimid sides, so their strength is left out. About 300 knights and 2,000 foot stayed to hold Palestine (Wikipedia). Winner: crusaders (0).
- **third-crusade**: both sides counted at Arsuf, 1191: crusaders c. 11,000–25,000 and Ayyubids c. 25,000–35,000 (Wikipedia, Battle of Arsuf: the infobox gives 11,200 and 25,000, the body 25,000 and 35,000). Rum: no strength given. Treaty of Jaffa 2 September 1192, a three-year truce; ransom 150,000 marks (Wikipedia, Third Crusade). Winner: `null`, result "Compromise peace", because Jerusalem was not retaken but the coast was.
- **mongol-conquests**: Mongols c. 95,000, the nominal strength in 1206 (95 units of a thousand, Wikipedia, Mongol Empire). There is no comparable figure for the many states attacked or for the Mamluks across the whole war, so their strength is left out. Tactics (decimal units, 3–5 mounts per rider, feigned retreat, Chinese and Muslim engineers, calculated terror) are from May, https://journals.gmu.edu/whc/article/download/4473/2440?inline=1. Ain Jalut: one tumen left under Kitbuqa (Wikipedia). Winner: Mongols (0), with the result line saying they were halted in 1260.
- **hundred-years-war**: both sides counted at Agincourt, 1415: English c. 6,000–9,000 (Wikipedia infobox 6,000–8,100; Curry c. 9,000) and French c. 12,000–15,000 (infobox 14,000–15,000 without armed servants; Curry c. 12,000). Castillon 17 July 1453 and Bordeaux 19 October 1453 (Wikipedia, Battle of Castillon). Standing army 1445; England left insolvent; Wars of the Roses (Wikipedia, Hundred Years' War). Winner: France (1).

## Disputed or hedged
- Plutarch's 60,000 for the Peloponnesian invasion army is an ancient figure and probably inflated, so it is hedged as "perhaps". The Athenian 29,000 adds the 13,000 field hoplites to the 16,000 garrison, which included the oldest and youngest citizens and metics.
- Polybius's Cannae figures are ancient. Modern estimates of the Roman losses vary a great deal, but the army sizes are the ones most widely quoted.
- First Crusade numbers vary widely (60,000 to 160,000 depending on whether non-combatants are counted). I used the range in the article body, which matches the "perhaps 100,000" in `content/wars`.
- Arsuf strengths are inconsistent even within one source, so both sides are given as wide ranges.
- The Mongol 95,000 is a nominal organisation in 1206, not a head count. Wikipedia also says armies in Genghis's lifetime were perhaps 50,000–75,000 strong in the field.
- Agincourt: Curry's records-based estimate (c. 9,000 English vs c. 12,000 French) is disputed by Barker, Sumption and Rogers, so ranges are used.
- The Hundred Years' War had no peace treaty in 1453. The Treaty of Picquigny (1475) is sometimes said to have formally ended it, so the result line says only "no peace was signed".

## Possible issues in `content/wars` / `content/cinema` (not edited)
- `cinema/mongol-conquests.json`, chapter `syria`, says news of Möngke's death made Hulagu withdraw. Wikipedia (Battle of Ain Jalut) reports that a Hulagu letter found in the 1980s blames logistics instead: fodder was exhausted and the army moved to cooler summer pastures. That reason is now disputed, so the chapter could say "news of Möngke's death and lack of fodder".
- `wars/a-early.json`, peloponnesian-war: the summary says the plague killed "perhaps a quarter" of Athens. Livius says about a third of the citizens. The figure is uncertain, so a range would be safer.
- `wars/a-early.json`, peloponnesian-war: side 0 ("Athens and the Delian League") lists only the Odrysian Kingdom as a member, with no Athens entry. This may be a gap in the map polygons, not an error.
- `wars/a-early.json`, peloponnesian-war: the summary gives the surrender as April 404 BCE. The Wikipedia Aegospotami page says March 404 BCE, and the main Wikipedia page says 25 April. This is a minor discrepancy, and I followed April.
- `wars/a-early.json`, mongol-conquests: the summary says that by 1260 the empire "stretched from Korea to Hungary". The Mongols withdrew from Hungary in 1242, and Korea's submission was still being completed in 1259–60. The brief uses "from northern China to Russia and Iran" instead.
