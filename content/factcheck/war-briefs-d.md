## Figures chosen
- **paraguayan-war**: Allies c. 26,000 at the start (Brazil c. 16,000 in its southern garrisons + Argentina c. 8,500 + Uruguay under 2,000); Paraguay c. 30,000–40,000 at the start (LOC Country Studies says c. 30,000; Wikipedia says 38,000 under arms). Population loss "perhaps 60–70%" (Whigham & Potthast, via Wikipedia). Treaties 1872 and 1876, occupation until 1876, Humaitá passed by ironclads on 19 Feb 1868 and fallen 25 Jul 1868, Brazilian army and the 1889 fall of the monarchy: all from Wikipedia. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguayan_War, https://countrystudies.us/paraguay/11.htm
- **first-italo-ethiopian-war**: at Adwa, Italy c. 17,700 and Ethiopia c. 73,000–100,000, both matching the cinema text. Menelik's rifle purchases and his march by Hawzen; Baratieri's estimate of 30,000 and his separated brigades; Crispi's fall. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Italo-Ethiopian_War. Treaty of Addis Ababa, 26 October 1896, from https://encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/addis-ababa-treaty
- **spanish-american-war**: US army c. 275,000 in 1898 (59,000 regulars + 216,000 volunteers, https://www.armyheritage.org/soldier-stories-information/the-spanish-american-war-manifest-destiny-goes-abroad/). Spain c. 260,000–340,000 in its colonies (Wikipedia infobox: 278,447 in Cuba, 10,005 in Puerto Rico, 51,331 in the Philippines; the low end uses c. 197,000 regulars and volunteers in Cuba). Ceasefire 12 Aug 1898; Treaty of Paris 10 Dec 1898 (https://history.state.gov/milestones/1866-1898/spanish-american-war).
- **world-war-i**: Allies c. 42 million mobilised (the sum of the Tucker figures on Wikipedia's Allies page, c. 41.7M); Central Powers c. 23–25 million (classic 22.85M; Wikipedia's Central Powers table gives 25.26M). Blockade from Wikipedia (Blockade of Germany); Hundred Days from https://www.theworldwar.org/learn/about-wwi/hundred-days-offensive
- **world-war-ii**: no strength figures (see below). Deaths 60–75 million (Wikipedia; National WWII Museum gives c. 15M battle + 45M civilian). Uranus encircled 250,000–300,000 Axis troops (Wikipedia). Island hopping and Rabaul from https://www.nationalww2museum.org/node/1038. Vichy leaders from Wikipedia.
- **arab-israeli-war-1948**: Israel c. 115,000 at its peak (Wikipedia infobox: 29,677 at first, later 117,500); Arab states c. 50,000–65,000 at their peak (infobox: 51,100–63,500 "later"). Territory c. 78%, armistice dates, refugees 700,000–750,000 (UNCCP: 711,000 outside Israel), and over 260,000 Jews from Arab countries reaching Israel by 1951: all from Wikipedia. Transjordan kept to Arab-state areas, and the lines held until 1967: https://history.state.gov/milestones/1945-1952/arab-israeli-war

## Disputed or hedged
- Paraguay: army size at the start ranges from 30,000 to 100,000 across sources, so I gave a hedged 30,000–40,000. Population loss is disputed: Reber's low estimate is c. 21,000 deaths, and Whigham/Potthast give 60–70%. I wrote "perhaps... by one widely cited estimate... historians still dispute". Territory lost is given as both "about 30%" and "almost 40%" in different parts of the Wikipedia article, so I left the percentage out.
- Ethiopia: Adwa estimates run from 73,000 to 120,000, and one source says over 100,000. I kept the cinema's 73,000–100,000. The treaty is dated 23 October in the Wikipedia infobox and 26 October in most sources; I used 26 October, which matches the cinema end date.
- Spanish–American War: Spanish strength is either a paper army-list figure or a deployed count, so I gave a wide range. The two sides' figures are not on quite the same basis: US army strength versus Spain's colonial garrisons.
- WWI: Central Powers total hedged as 23–25 million because the source tables differ.
- WWII: I found no credible, comparable whole-coalition total for the Allies, the Axis or Vichy, so `strength` and `strengthWhat` are left out for all three sides. (The Vichy Armistice Army was capped at c. 94,000 men, which is not comparable.)
- 1948: no credible single figure exists for Palestinian Arab forces (estimates of Arab combatants in the civil war run from c. 12,000 to c. 23,500 and mix in the Arab Liberation Army), so their strength is left out. The Arab states' peak figure partly carries "citation needed" tags on Wikipedia and is hedged as a range. Plan Dalet is described by what it did, without taking a side in the debate over intent (Tal and Gelber versus Khalidi and Pappé; Morris in between).
