# War playback: War of 1812 and Spanish–American War

Output: two war objects (`war-of-1812`, `spanish-american-war`), staged in the session scratchpad as
`wars-1812-1898.json` for the coordinator to merge into `content/wars/`. No other repo file was edited.

## War of 1812 (1812–1815)

### Member choices
- **United States:** `United States of America` 1812–1815 (rows 1812–1813, 1814, 1815–1819).
- **Britain:** `(British Empire)` 1812–1815, per WARS.md. It covers `Kingdom of Great Britain` (drawn as
  "United Kingdom" from 1801 after the corrections relabel), `British Colonial Empire` (Lower Canada, Nova
  Scotia, New Brunswick, Bermuda, Jamaica) and `British Empire` (the Pacific Northwest shape), since all three
  have `memberOf: ["(British Empire)"]`.
- **Left out, described in the text only:** Tecumseh's confederacy and the other Indigenous nations allied to
  Britain (Shawnee, Odawa, Ojibwe, Menominee, Ho-Chunk, Sioux, Wyandot, Mohawk), the Red Stick Creeks, and the
  Cherokee and Lower Creek allies of the United States. None of them has a realm in index.json or a region in
  supplements.json.
- **Spain** (Pensacola, Spanish Florida) was neutral, so it is not a member, although a few Spanish soldiers were
  killed when Jackson took Pensacola in November 1814.
- **moment:** null. No moment in `content/moments*.json` covers the war.

### Map gaps noticed (for maintainers)
- **Upper Canada is not drawn in 1812–1815.** Point checks on the 1800 chunk show no polygon at York (Toronto),
  Kingston or Queenston/Niagara. Several events happen there (Queenston Heights, York, Fort George, Lundy's Lane,
  the Thames, Crysler's Farm), so their pins fall on unshaded land. Only Lower Canada and the Maritimes are
  British (`British Colonial Empire`).
- **The US interior west of the Appalachians is only partly drawn.** Mackinac, Chicago, Tippecanoe, Prairie du
  Chien and Horseshoe Bend (Mississippi Territory/Alabama) fall outside any polygon. Detroit, Mobile and New
  Orleans are inside `United States of America`.
- **Pensacola** is drawn as `United States of America` in 1814 and as `Spanish Empire` in 1812–13 and 1815. That
  roughly fits Jackson's brief seizure of Pensacola in November 1814, but the snapshot covers the whole year.
- **Fort Astoria** (mouth of the Columbia) is drawn as `British Empire` from 1814 on. That is plausible: the post
  was sold to the North West Company in 1813 and taken over by the Royal Navy. The war data does not depend on it.
- **Eastern Maine** (Castine, occupied September 1814 to April 1815) is drawn as US throughout, which is expected
  at this granularity.

### Contested or uncertain facts and how they were handled
- **Casualties:** the main Wikipedia article gives two different British figures (table: about 2,700 killed and
  about 10,000 from all causes; prose: about 1,160 killed and 3,321 dead of disease). The text says "up to about
  10,000". US figures are 2,200 (infobox) or 2,260 (prose) killed in action and about 15,000 from all causes. The
  Indigenous figure is the infobox's "10,000 Indigenous warriors and civilians dead from all causes".
- **Ratification:** the Battle of New Orleans article says the US ratified on 16 February 1815 and the War of
  1812 article says 17 February. The text uses "Senate approved 16 February, ratifications exchanged 17
  February", with the label "16–17 Feb 1815" and the end date 17 February.
- **Châteauguay:** the fetched War of 1812 text dates it 25 October 1813, while many sources give 26 October. It
  is folded into the Crysler's Farm event as "in late October", so no exact date is shown.
- **Frenchtown/River Raisin:** "about 60" killed prisoners is the article's figure, given "by some accounts".
  The killings were on 23 January, the day after the battle.
- **Fort Mims:** the main article gives 400 killed in one place and 400–500 in another. The text uses "some 400
  to 500".
- **Detroit prisoners:** the infobox gives 2,188 captured and the body gives 589 regulars plus 1,606 militia.
  The text uses "more than 2,000".

## Spanish–American War (1898)

### Member choices
- **United States:** `United States of America` 1898 (row 1898–1899).
- **Cuban revolutionaries:** `Cuban Nationalists` 1898 (rows 1895–1897 and 1898–1899, no corrections entry, no
  `memberOf`). Unlike Tecumseh's confederacy, the Cuban insurgents *do* have a realm, so they are a member of the
  US side ("United States, with Cuban and Filipino revolutionaries"). In the 1898 snapshot it covers central
  Cuba (Camagüey). Note: its Wikidata/wiki link in index.json is "Fidel Castro", a Cliopatria mislink; the shape
  is clearly the 1895–98 Cuban War of Independence insurgent territory. A maintainer may want to fix the realm's
  wiki link or note.
- **Filipino revolutionaries (Aguinaldo):** no realm, so they are left out of members and described in the
  text (Kawit declaration, siege of Manila, exclusion from the city on 13 August).
- **Spain:** `(Spanish Empire)` 1898 (rows 1898–1899). In 1898 the group consists only of `Kingdom of Spain`
  (`memberOf: ["(Spanish Empire)"]`, same area, 1,230,176 km²), which holds peninsular Spain, Havana and western
  Cuba, Puerto Rico, most of the Philippines and the African territories. The empire group was preferred, as WARS.md
  recommends for colonial powers. `Spanish Empire` (no parentheses) has no 1898 row, so it cannot be used.
  The `Kingdom of Spain` crop for 1899 (Spain and Africa only) does not affect a 1898-only member.
- **Hawaii** was annexed during the war but was not a belligerent, so it is not a member. `Republic of Hawai'i`
  rows end in 1897, and Honolulu is inside `United States of America` in the 1898 snapshot.
- **moment:** null. No moment covers the war (the only 1898 mention is in the Spanish American wars of independence
  moment's "why" text).

### Map gaps noticed (for maintainers)
- **A single 1898–1899 snapshot already has the war's results.** Point checks on the 1875 chunk (feature
  years 1898–1899) put Santiago de Cuba (eastern Cuba), Manila and Guam inside `United States of America`
  for the whole of 1898, while Havana, San Juan, Ponce, central Luzon, Cebu and Mindanao are `Kingdom of Spain`.
  So before May–July 1898 (and before the 15 February Maine event) the playback shows Manila, Guam and the
  Santiago area as American, and Puerto Rico stays Spanish even after the July–August invasion. This cannot
  be fixed with a member date: the realm row is 1898–1899 in one piece. A corrections split by year (or a
  per-month war overlay) would be needed.
- **Puerto Rico in 1899** belongs to no polygon once the 1899 `Kingdom of Spain` crop applies (the crop keeps
  only Spain and Africa), which leaves it unclaimed rather than American. The same applies to the Spanish-held
  parts of Cuba and the Philippines. The war ends in 1898, so this does not affect playback, but the 1899 map
  is incomplete.
- Guam is drawn as American while the rest of the Marianas and the Carolines (sold to Germany in 1899) do not
  appear as separate shapes; too small to matter at playback zoom.

### Contested or uncertain facts and how they were handled
- **Blockade date:** the Office of the Historian gives 22 April; Wikipedia gives 21 April (the date Congress
  later declared war to have begun). The event is dated 22 April, and the text says war was declared on 25 April
  "since 21 April".
- **USS Maine:** the cause is still disputed (Sampson board 1898: external mine; Spanish inquiry: internal coal
  fire; Vreeland board 1911: external; Rickover study 1976: internal coal-bunker fire). The text gives both 1898
  findings and says the cause is still debated. Deaths: 260 killed outright and a few later. The article gives
  261 in one place and 266–268 in others, so the text says "about 260 ... a few more died later".
- **Casualties:** Wikipedia infobox: US 2,446 dead (385 killed in action, 2,061 disease); Spain 15,700–15,800
  (about 700–800 killed, about 15,000 disease). Cuban and Filipino losses are not given; the text says they are
  not well recorded.
- **Santiago naval battle prisoners:** 1,612 (Spanish–American War article) vs about 1,889 (battle article);
  the text says "some 1,600 to 1,900".
- **San Juan Hill casualties:** the battle article gives 144 killed, 1,024 wounded and 72 missing for the
  heights. The text says "more than 1,000 killed and wounded" for the day.
- **Battle of Manila casualties:** the article gives 19 US killed and 103 wounded in one place and 6 killed in
  another. The text says men were killed on both sides, with no number.
- **Puerto Rico casualties:** sources differ (9 killed and 46 wounded, or 3 dead and 40 wounded). The text says
  only "few casualties".
- **Protocol terms** ("an island in the Marianas"; the Philippines left to the peace conference) and the Hawaii
  transfer date (12 August in the Newlands article text; its image caption says 23 August for a flag-raising) are
  stated from the fetched pages and the treaty's well-known text.
