# Fact-check: profiles-gaps-3a (Jin 266–420, Cao Wei, Eastern Wu, Former Qin, Northern Wei, Northern Zhou, Liu Song, Southern Qi, Liang)

Peak areas were checked against the Taagepera 1979 figures cited in Wikipedia's "List of largest empires" table (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_empires). All of them match: Western Jin 3.1M (280), Eastern Jin 2.8M (347), Cao Wei 2.0M (263), Former Qin 2.0M (376), Liu Song 2.0M (450), Northern Wei 2.0M (450), Eastern Wu 1.5M (221), Northern Zhou 1.5M (577), Liang 1.3M (502/549). The Turchin, Adams & Hall (2006) Liang figure (1.30M km², dated 579) was confirmed in the JWSR PDF table.

## Writer's doubts, resolved

- Northern Zhou peak: Taagepera does give 1.5M km² for 577, the same as Northern Qi (557). The figure is kept with a hedge in the note.
- Liang peak year: Taagepera dates 1.3M to 502 and 549, and Turchin et al. give 1.30M for 579 (confirmed in their PDF). 579 falls after the dynasty ended and probably reflects the Western Liang rump. The note is unchanged.
- Eastern Wu 221 vs 222: 221 is Taagepera's date. It is kept, and the note now explains it.
- Eastern Jin founding: Sima Rui was King of Jin in 317 and emperor in 318. The ruler reign is now 318–323, and capital "from 317" stays because Wikipedia gives Jiankang as capital 317–420.
- Emperor Ming of Liu Song: he reigned 9 January 466 – 10 May 472, so "466 – 472" is correct.
- Fall of Jiangling: Emperor Yuan's reign ended 7 January 555 and he died 27 January 555 (late 554 in the Chinese calendar). "555" is correct in Western dating.
- 870,000 at the Fei River: this is the Book of Jin figure. A 2012 study judges the battle's scale "dramatically exaggerated", which the text already says.

- [northern-zhou], [liang-dynasty] "this map draws it larger / much larger": not checkable against snapshot names. Left as written.
