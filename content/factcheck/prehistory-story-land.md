Merges, made to keep within 13 chapters while covering every required topic:
- "Bare land before plants (microbial crusts)" and "first land plants (spores c. 470 Ma)" are one chapter (1). Both
  belong to the same moment and the evidence for microbial life on land (lake beds about 1,000 Ma, weathering about
  1,200 Ma, per Wikipedia "Evolutionary history of plants") lies far outside the story span, so a separate chapter would
  have needed an anchor date inside the span with no find spot of its own.
- "Fungi and the first soils" is split between chapter 4 (Rhynie chert: oldest mycorrhizal fungi, Prototaxites) and
  chapter 5 (forests: roots weathering rock and carrying carbon into soils; Archaeopteris roots in the Cairo palaeosol).
- The Zachełmie trackways are a second site in the Tiktaalik chapter (6) rather than their own chapter, with the
  dispute in evidenceNote; this keeps the "disputed earlier sign" next to the fossil it challenges.
- Giant griffinflies (Meganeura, about 304 to 299 Ma, Commentry) are a second site (ma 302) in the winged-insect chapter (10).
- Synapsids (Archaeothyris, Florence NS, about 307 Ma) are mentioned in the closing chapter (13). o:conifers is not
  referenced: I found no page I opened that ties early conifers to the collapse, so I left them out rather than assert it.

## Dates and how they agree with the project

- Ch 1, 470 Ma: agrees with o:land-plants (fossilMa 470). Rubinstein et al. 2010 give Dapingian, c. 473 to 471 Ma; site ma 471.
- Ch 2, 427 Ma: agrees with o:vascular-plants (427). Garza et al. 2024 (Geological Magazine) give maximum depositional
  ages of 426 ± 2 Ma (Borrisnoe Mountain, Hollyford Formation, Co. Tipperary) and 427 ± 2 Ma (Capel Horeb, Wales).
  Note: Wikipedia "Cooksonia" gives Wenlock, about 433 Ma, for the earliest specimens and about 432 Ma for
  C. barrandei from Czechia (named in 2018); the text mentions the Czech plant as "may be about 432 million
  years old". The UCC page says "about 415 million years" and "a few millimetres tall"; I followed Wikipedia
  ("a few centimetres tall") and the 2024 zircon ages instead, as the UCC figure is a loose period label.
- Ch 3, 425 Ma: Kampecaris on Kerrera dated about 425 Ma (Brookfield et al. 2020, reported by UT Austin). Pneumodesmus
  re-dated to about 414 Ma (Suarez et al. 2017); Wikipedia notes a 2023 study proposing about 430 Ma and a 2025 review
  favouring Pridoli to Lochkovian, so 414 is kept as the zircon age. Dolichophonus 436 to 428 Ma (site ma 432, midpoint).
- Ch 4, 410 Ma: agrees with e:rhynie-chert (410). o:fungi uses 407 Ma (radiometric age); Wikipedia text says about 410.
- Ch 5, 390 Ma: agrees with o:first-trees (390). Cairo forest: Stein et al. 2020 give mid-Givetian; site ma 386 is my
  estimate within that range (Wikipedia "Calamophyton" says Somerset predates Cairo by about four million years). Gilboa 385 agrees with e:gilboa-forest.
- Ch 6, 375 Ma: agrees with e:tiktaalik. Zachełmie site ma 395: the Wikipedia village article gives "395 million
  years"; the trackways article gives early to middle Eifelian (on the current ICS chart about 393 to 390 Ma);
  o:tetrapods uses 390 for the same tracks. I used 395, as the brief asked, with "about" in the text; the difference is
  only a timescale revision.
- Ch 7, 372 Ma: agrees with e:late-devonian-extinction (372; Hangenberg about 359).
- Ch 8, 365 Ma: Wikipedia gives Acanthostega about 365 Ma, Ichthyostega 365 to 360 Ma (Famennian).
- Ch 9, 365 Ma: agrees with o:seed-plants (365); Wikipedia "Seed" gives "around 365 million years old" for Elkinsia.
- Ch 10, 325 Ma: agrees with o:winged-insects (325); Wikipedia "Pterygota" gives 328 to 324 Ma.
- Ch 11, 315 Ma: agrees with e:joggins (315, range 318 to 315). o:amniotes and Wikipedia "Hylonomus" use 318 Ma.
- Ch 12, 309 Ma: agrees with e:mazon-creek.
- Ch 13, 305 Ma: Wikipedia "Carboniferous rainforest collapse" gives about 305 Ma (end Moscovian to early Kasimovian).
  The Riola/Vermilion Grove forest lies on the Desmoinesian Herrin (No. 6) Coal; the Illinois Mines wiki says "about
  300 million years old" loosely, and a search snippet of a Smithsonian article (which I could not open, HTTP 403)
  says 307 Ma. I gave the site ma 307 and the text gives no number for the forest, only "not long afterwards".

## Disputed points (both sides)

1. Earliest land plants (ch 1). Accepted: cryptospores about 470 Ma from Argentina (Rubinstein et al. 2010), with
   Darriwilian spores from Saudi Arabia and Czechia. Disputed: older spore-like fossils (Wikipedia "Cryptospore" cites
   Strother 2024 for Middle Cambrian cryptospores; o:land-plants notes 480 Ma claims) that may come from algae on
   the way to land plants rather than land plants. Wikipedia "Cryptospore" itself states both "middle of the Cambrian"
   and "around 470 million years ago" without reconciling them.
2. Prototaxites (ch 4). Long held to be a giant fungus; Loron et al. (Science Advances, January 2026; University of
   Edinburgh page) argue P. taiti from the Rhynie chert lacks chitin and fungal biomarkers and belongs to an extinct
   eukaryote lineage. The study covers one species from one site; the fungus view is not dead. Wikipedia "Rhynie chert"
   still says its affinities are debated. Text says "a 2026 study argued".
3. Zachełmie trackways (ch 6). Niedźwiedzki, Ahlberg and colleagues (Nature 2010) and Qvarnström et al. (2018) treat
   them as tetrapod tracks made in ephemeral lakes; Lucas (2015, 2019) argued for fish nests or feeding traces; Shubin
   suggested some digit-less prints may come from walking fish. A 2022 study (cited in Wikipedia "Tiktaalik") calls them
   "largely accepted". evidence = disputed.
4. Causes of the Late Devonian extinctions (ch 7). Plant-nutrient hypothesis (Algeo and Scheckler 1998), volcanism,
   cooling/glaciation and sea-level fall (UCMP), impacts or CO2 change (UCMP), supernova for the Hangenberg (Fields et
   al. 2020, in e:late-devonian-extinction). Severity also contested (Wikipedia: about 40 percent of marine species, with
   uncertainty). evidence = disputed.
5. Hylonomus and the first amniotes (ch 11). Classic oldest reptile (Dawson); a 2025 analysis (Wikipedia "Hylonomus",
   "Amniote") places it outside crown Amniota. Long et al. (Nature 2025) claim 350 Ma amniote footprints from the Snowy
   Plains Formation, Victoria; Wikipedia "Amniote" notes the trackmaker may not have been an amniote. o:amniotes gives
   355 Ma for these tracks; Wikipedia "Amniote" gives about 350. I used 350 in the text, following the page I opened.
6. Rainforest collapse and tetrapods (ch 13). Sahney, Benton and Falcon-Lang (Geology 2010) argued the collapse made
   isolated "islands" and drove tetrapod diversification; Dunne et al. (Proc. R. Soc. B 2018), after correcting for
   sampling, found no endemism signal and instead more widespread communities in the early Permian. evidence = disputed.
7. Oxygen and giant griffinflies (ch 10). Harlé (1911) and later work link size to high Carboniferous oxygen; a Late
   Permian giant at a time of lower oxygen, and a 2026 study by Snelling and colleagues (Wikipedia "Meganeura"), argue
   against. Text: "A popular idea ... but some recent studies doubt the link." Evidence for the chapter stays "fossil".
8. Molecular clocks vs fossils: millipedes (about 75 million years older than Kampecaris by gene clocks) and winged
   insects (about 400 Ma by gene clocks, Wikipedia "Evolution of insects"; "likely analytical artefacts" per Wikipedia
   "Pterygota"). Mentioned in evidenceNotes.

## Left out, and why

- Rhyniognatha (Rhynie chert, once "the first insect"): identity disputed (Haug & Haug 2017, myriapod?); already in
  o:insects, left out to keep chapter 4 short.
- Baragwanathia (Australia, about 425 Ma) and Guiyu: belong to other groups' stories; not needed for the land narrative.
- Guandacol Formation, Argentina, winged insects (upper Serpukhovian, 325 to 324 Ma): I saw it only in search
  snippets and Wikipedia "Evolution of insects" (which names a species, "Xenoptera latigra", that a search could not
  confirm), so I did not cite it. A future pass could add it as a second oldest-winged-insect site.
- Arthropleura as its own chapter: mentioned at Mazon Creek; image arthropleura-beach is reused there.
- Elginerpeton (oldest tetrapod bones, Scotland, 375 Ma): already in o:tetrapods.
- Exact percentages for Carboniferous oxygen (Wikipedia gives "up to 80% above modern levels"); left out as the
  number varies between models.
- The Smithsonian article on the Riola/Vermilion forest (HTTP 403) and the USGS abstract page (empty) could not be read;
  I relied on the Illinois Mines wiki (ISGS) only, and did not use the snippet claim that two-thirds of its species vanished.
