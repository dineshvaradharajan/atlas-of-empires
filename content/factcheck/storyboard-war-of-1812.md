## Chapters and why
1. **The fall of Detroit** (18 Jun – 20 Aug 1812): Hull's crossing to Sandwich (12 Jul), Fort Mackinac (17 Jul), Hull's withdrawal (7–8 Aug), Fort Dearborn (15 Aug), Detroit surrendered (16 Aug), Michigan occupied.
2. **The Niagara frontier, 1812–1813** (12 Oct 1812 – 31 Dec 1813): Queenston Heights, York (27 Apr – 8 May 1813), Fort George (27 May), the brief American hold on the whole peninsula, Stoney Creek, Beaver Dams, the Fort George perimeter, Newark, Fort Niagara (19 Dec), Lewiston and Buffalo raids.
3. **Lake Erie and the Thames** (18 Jan – 12 Oct 1813): Frenchtown (18 and 22 Jan), Fort Meigs, Lake Erie (10 Sep), Harrison's landing (27 Sep), Detroit retaken (29–30 Sep), the Thames (5 Oct), Harrison back to Detroit keeping Amherstburg and Sandwich.
4. **The Creek War** (27 Jul 1813 – 9 Aug 1814): Burnt Corn, Fort Mims, Jackson's advance (Fort Strother, Tallushatchee, Talladega), Horseshoe Bend, Fort Jackson and the cession.
5. **Chippawa, Lundy's Lane and Fort Erie** (3 Jul – 5 Nov 1814).
6. **The occupation of eastern Maine** (11 Jul – 15 Sep 1814): Moose Island, Castine, Belfast, Hampden, Bangor, Machias, then the coast east of the Penobscot held to the end.
7. **Bladensburg and the burning of Washington** (19 Aug – 2 Sep 1814), with the Alexandria surrender.
8. **Plattsburgh** (31 Aug – 16 Sep 1814).
9. **Baltimore** (11–16 Sep 1814).
10. **New Orleans** (10 Dec 1814 – 17 Feb 1815): Lake Borgne, the landing at Villeré's, 8 January, withdrawal, Fort Bowyer (12 Feb), ratification (17 Feb).

Left out (no chapter): the Montreal campaign of 1813 (Châteauguay, Crysler's Farm: no ground changed hands), Sackets Harbor, Prairie du Chien and the 1814 Mackinac attack, the 1813 Chesapeake raids, the naval duels and the first attack on Fort Bowyer (Sep 1814). The British hold on Mackinac (to the end) and Fort Niagara (to the end) are still drawn.

## How it is drawn
- Sides: 0 = United States; 1 = Britain, its colonies and Indigenous allies (the Red Stick Creeks are drawn on this side; see hardest calls).
- `baseYears`: 1813 borders throughout. The 1814 data shades the Fort Jackson cession and Pensacola as American from January 1814 and part of the Thames valley as American; the 1815 data flips it back.
- **Upper Canada** is not drawn by the base map (York, Kingston, Chippawa, Fort Erie, Amherstburg, Moraviantown sit on unshaded land), so five British areas cover it: the Western District, the London District and Lake Erie shore, the Niagara peninsula, York and the Lake Ontario shore, Kingston and the St Lawrence. They overlap each other slightly (one continuous British area), and their edges follow the Detroit/St Clair rivers, the Niagara and the St Lawrence. American lodgements are cut out of them while they last (Sandwich 1812, Queenston 13 Oct 1812, York 1813, Fort George 1813, Brown's army 1814, the Detroit frontier strip from Sep 1813).
- **US blanks and base errors**: the base draws Lewiston and the Queenston gorge (New York bank) British and leaves Buffalo blank, so a US area covers the New York bank of the Niagara (cut back during the December 1813 raids). Michigan's Lake St Clair shore and the River Raisin are blank, so after 29 Sep 1813 a "Michigan Territory retaken" US area covers them. Fort Mims/Tensaw, Huntsville and the Creek country are blank: US areas for Tensaw, Jackson's advance and the cession; Red Stick area for the Upper Creek towns. Mackinac Island, Fort George and Fort Bowyer fall in water on the coarse coastline, so small areas carry them (Fort Mackinac US to 17 Jul 1812; Fort Bowyer US until 11 Feb 1815).
- British raids and occupations on US ground: Michigan (16 Aug 1812 – 29 Sep 1813), River Raisin, Fort Niagara (19 Dec 1813 to the end), the Lewiston and Buffalo raids, eastern Maine, Ross's corridor Benedict–Washington, Alexandria, Prevost's corridor to Plattsburgh, North Point, the camp below New Orleans, Fort Bowyer.

## Uncertainties and choices (no claim depends on an estimated date)
- Dates estimated, not found in the fetched pages: Harrison's withdrawal from Moraviantown to Detroit (keyed 7–12 Oct 1813); the end of the Buffalo raid (area kept to 1 Jan 1814); the end of the Lewiston raid (Riall back across by 27 Dec, sourced only as "returned to the Canadian side"); Ross's march back to Benedict (26–29 Aug 1814); Prevost back at the border (14–15 Sep 1814); Jackson's arrival at the Hickory Ground / Fort Jackson (keyed 18 Apr 1814) and the Red Stick collapse (area gone by 18 Apr); the Tensaw settlement around Fort Mims shown abandoned 30 Aug – 2 Sep 1813 only.
- Machias: the Machias article dates the occupation 1–4 Sep 1814, the Hampden article has a squadron moving on Machias from 26 Aug, the main article "September"; the area reaches Machias on 11 Sep and its claim is dated 15 Sep.
- Bangor entered 5 Sep and left 6 Sep (Hampden article); Hampden and Belfast are drawn held 2–7 Sep.
- Fort George and Fort Niagara are 1.3 km apart across the river mouth; the Fort George claim point is set about 250 m west of the article coordinate so the river line can pass between them. Lewiston's claim was dropped for the same reason (its coordinate is within 100 m of the river line).
- Villeré plantation's position is estimated from "nine miles south of the city, one mile from the Mississippi"; the British camp area is approximate.
- Casualties kept as ranges where sources differ (Fort George, Lundy's Lane, Bladensburg, North Point, the Thames). Fort Mims: about 500 killed or captured (infobox: 265 militia and 252 civilians). Plattsburgh strength: 11,000 in the force, "just over 8,000" present; text says "more than 8,000".
- Frenchtown: 397–410 American dead in the sources; text and battle say "about 400".

## Warnings left
"Little visible change" in chapters 2 (Niagara 1812–13), 5 (Niagara 1814), 7 (Washington), 8 (Plattsburgh), 9 (Baltimore) and 10 (New Orleans). In each, ground is taken and given back inside the chapter: the Americans hold Queenston for a day, York for two weeks and the Niagara peninsula for a summer, then leave; Brown's army takes and abandons the Canadian bank; Ross, Prevost and Brooke advance and withdraw within days; Pakenham's army lands and re-embarks. The start and end frames are therefore close (only Fort Niagara, the Buffalo raid and Fort Bowyer differ), while the keyframes in between show the change. The areas involved are also small (a river bank, a march corridor), under the 1,500 km² threshold even at their peak.
