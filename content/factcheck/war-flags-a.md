# War flags, slice A

Each flag was checked against its Wikimedia Commons description page, which had to say it is that flag for that period. Licences are recorded in content/war-flags/<war>.json.

- **american-civil-war**: Union: 34-star US flag (in use 4 July 1861 to 3 July 1863, the flag at the start of the war). Confederacy: the first national flag, the "Stars and Bars", in its 13-star form (Dec 1861 to May 1863), the national flag in widest use. The battle flag was left out because it was never the national flag. The border states had no separate flag.
- **american-revolutionary-war**: United States: 13-star flag (1777–1795, stars 3-2-3-2-3, as the Commons page describes it; this avoids the disputed "Betsy Ross" circle). France: the royal standard (blue with gold fleurs-de-lis, used c.1638–1790). Spain: Cross of Burgundy, the Spanish military flag, because the red-yellow flag only dates from 1785. Dutch Republic: Statenvlag (red-white-blue States flag). Britain: the pre-1801 Union Flag (1707–1800). Hessian/German contingents, Loyalists and Native nations were skipped: they had no attested flag of the period (the modern Hesse flag would be anachronistic).
- **arab-israeli-war-1948**: Israel: the Star of David flag, adopted Oct 1948 and flown by the Zionist movement and the Yishuv before that. Palestinian Arab forces: none, because neither the Army of the Holy War nor the Arab Liberation Army has a flag well attested on Commons. Arab League: Kingdom of Egypt (green crescent flag, 1922–1958), Transjordan (the Jordanian flag, unchanged since 1928), Kingdom of Iraq (1924–1959), Syria (independence flag, 1930–1958). Lebanon and Saudi Arabia were left out to keep the limit of 4.
- **conquest-of-the-aztec-empire**: Spain: Cross of Burgundy, the Habsburg Spanish military flag from Charles V's time, used in New Spain. Tlaxcala and other Indigenous allies, and the Mexica, were skipped: modern renderings of Mexica or Tlaxcalan "flags" are reconstructions, not attested period flags.
- **first-crusade**: none on any side. No crusader, Byzantine, Seljuk or Fatimid flag of 1096–1099 is attested; Commons versions are modern inventions.
- **first-italo-ethiopian-war**: Italy: Kingdom of Italy tricolour with the Savoy cross (1861–1946). Ethiopia: none. The Commons tricolour flags are dated 1897 or later (after Adwa, 1896), and the 1875–1881 flag belongs to Yohannes IV, not to Menelik's army.
- **hundred-years-war**: England: the royal standard of the quartered arms of France modern and England (1406–1603), the banner of the Lancastrian phase from Agincourt to 1453. The 1340–1406 version with France ancient has no banner-shaped file on Commons, only a shield. France: three gold fleurs-de-lis on blue (France modern, Commons "14th–16th centuries"). Scotland: Royal Banner of Scotland (lion rampant). Castile: royal banner of the Crown of Castile, quartered castles and lions (early style). Burgundy was skipped: the only Cross of Burgundy file on Commons is described as the Spanish/Habsburg flag. Gascony and the Welsh had no separate flag.
- **imjin-war**: none on any side. The Hinomaru was not a national flag of Japan in 1592 (Hideyoshi's armies fought under daimyo clan standards), Joseon had no national flag (the Taegukgi dates from 1882), and Ming China had no national flag.
- **korean-war**: UN side: South Korea (1949–1984 Taegukgi), United States (48 stars, 1912–1959), United Nations flag, United Kingdom (Union Flag). The other UN members were left out to keep the limit of 4. Communist side: North Korea (1948 flag), People's Republic of China (1949 flag, carried by the "Volunteers"), Soviet Union (1936–1955 flag).
- **mexican-american-war**: United States: 28-star flag (4 July 1846 to 3 July 1847, covering the war's opening and most of its campaigns). Bear Flag rebels: the original 1846 Bear Flag of the California Republic, a public-domain rendering of the original, not the later Todd/1911 state design. The Pío Pico sketch variant was not used because Commons says it was probably never made as a cloth flag. Mexico: the 1823 republican tricolour (1823–1864).
