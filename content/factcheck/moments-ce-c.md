## Figures and dates: which estimate was used
- Palestinian displacement 1947–49: "an estimated 700,000 to 750,000". The expulsion-and-flight article's
  lead says "more than 700,000" (body: "around 700,000", precise number "a matter of dispute"); the 1948 war
  and Nakba articles say "over 750,000" / "around 750,000"; the UN Conciliation Commission estimated
  711,000 refugees outside Israel in September 1949. The UN Nakba page says "more than half of the
  Palestinian population". Text says "about half the Arab population of Palestine".
- Villages: sources range from "400 to 600 villages destroyed" to "over 500 towns, villages and
  neighbourhoods depopulated" and "over 530". Text says "hundreds of Arab towns and villages were depopulated".
- 1948 war deaths: Israel 6,373 (about 1% of population). Arab deaths unknown (one estimate 7,000;
  infobox Arab armies 3,700–7,000 plus Palestinians 3,000–13,000). Text says "estimates in the thousands".
- Jewish departures from Arab states: "a similar number ... in the three years following the war, 260,000
  of whom went to Israel" (1948 war article lead).
- Iranian Revolution deaths (in "also"): Islamic Republic figure 60,000; Tucker's historians' consensus
  532–2,781; Kurzman 2,000–3,000. Text: "from about 500 to 3,000".
- Rwanda: user-requested mainstream range 500,000–800,000. The 800,000 figure is the UN expert's population-
  loss estimate for April–July 1994, which HRW notes includes non-genocide deaths; HRW's own estimate is at
  least 500,000 (507,000 Tutsi, about 77% of the Tutsi population); Wikipedia: "most scholarly estimates"
  500,000–662,000 Tutsi; Rwandan government 1,074,017; the UN Outreach page says "more than one million".
  Main text keeps 500,000–800,000 "most of them Tutsi"; the other figures are in "also".
- Rwanda end date: Wikipedia gives 7 April – 19 July; RPF took Kigali 4 July and the north-west 18 July.
  The UN page says killings continued until 4 July "in other areas". Text: Kigali 4 July, north-west by 18 July.
- Rwanda rape figures: UN 150,000–250,000; Wikipedia 250,000–500,000. Text: "hundreds of thousands".
- Paris Agreement parties: UN page says 195 Parties (194 States + EU); Wikipedia says 194 UNFCCC members
  are parties as of January 2026 (after the US withdrawal took effect). Text avoids a number: "almost every
  country has joined".
- Ukraine military casualties: Wikipedia casualties article lead, "between 1,274,000 and 2 million estimated
  casualties (killed and wounded)" from 24 February 2022 to June 2026. Text: "about 1.3 million to 2 million
  by mid-2026, though all such figures are uncertain".
- Ukraine civilians: OHCHR verified 17,257 civilians killed by 31 August 2026 and believes the real number
  is higher. Text: "more than 17,000 ... which it believes is an undercount".
- Donbas 2014–2021: 14,200–14,400 deaths. Text: "about 14,000 people before 2022".
- Ukraine refugees 6–7 million abroad; Russian occupation "roughly 20%". Both from the war article lead.

## Disputed or softened (contested politics)
- 1948 / Nakba: written so both narratives appear in the authors' own terms (the war article quotes Shlaim:
  "each side subscribes to a different version of events"). Israel's War of Independence and the Nakba are
  each named as their community calls them. Used "fled or were expelled" (the wording of all three
  Wikipedia articles) and did not adopt the label "ethnic cleansing", which the Nakba article uses and the
  expulsion article says scholars "widely" apply "though some disagree". The causes of the flight (expulsion
  versus flight) are debated, so no single cause is given. The refugee question is described as "a key
  issue in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict" (expulsion article) without saying whose position is right.
  The pin is at Independence Hall, Tel Aviv (site of the proclamation), not at a contested location
  such as Jerusalem.
- Rwanda: who shot down Habyarimana's plane is disputed (RPF vs Hutu extremists), so the text says
  "responsibility is still disputed". Whether the genocide was planned before 6 April is debated (the ICTR
  could not prove it), so the text does not claim prior planning.
- Iran: grievances against the Shah are given as perceptions ("many Iranians saw as ..."). The Ettela'at
  article is described as "government-planted" (Wikipedia: written under a pseudonym by a government agent).
- Ukraine: the war is ongoing (Wikipedia: peace negotiations stalled), so yearLabel is "2022 onwards" and
  all casualty figures are dated and hedged. Russia's stated justifications are not repeated; the text
  quotes only Putin's term "special military operation". The ICC warrants are mentioned without detail.
- Paris Agreement: US withdrawal in 2026 and "2024 hottest year on record, above 1.5°C" come from the
  current Wikipedia article (the latter is a single-year figure, not the Agreement's 20-year average).
